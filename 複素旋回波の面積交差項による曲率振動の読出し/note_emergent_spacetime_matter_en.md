# How Do Space, Time, and Matter Emerge from a Collection of Complex Numbers?

--- From inflation-like rapid expansion to vacuum, spacetime, and localized matter

Noriaki Kihara

## It does not all begin from an empty zero

What I have been investigating through numerical experiments is this problem: if, at a stage where there is not yet any space, any time, or even any notion of how many dimensions there are, a collection of complex numbers with extremely high symmetry exists, then what can emerge from it?

In my earlier inflation experiments, I found that when this highly symmetric state is given an extremely small fluctuation --- an asymmetry of roughly 10^-31 --- it is amplified explosively, producing an enormous spread on the order of 10^30.

And after that rapid change settled down, something curious remained: a structure that could be read as having three-dimensional spatial directions.

In the two papers presented here, I investigated this mechanism further, mathematically, algebraically, and geometrically.

As a result, it has become clear that from a highly symmetric collection of complex numbers, a nearly uniform vacuum of complex waves emerges, and from it a spacetime of three spatial dimensions plus one time dimension can be read out.

Furthermore, when a part of that uniform wave condenses locally, a structure appears there that can be read as a material entity, with a center and a finite extent.

In other words, a single continuous structure has come into view:

- a highly symmetric complex state
- inflation-like rapid expansion
- three-dimensional spatial directions
- four-dimensional spacetime
- a homogeneous vacuum
- localized matter

And this time, something even more unexpected became clear.

The internal states of the complex numbers are not themselves what we observe as our space and time.

The internal complex states become structures observed as space and time only through a readout: a logarithmic (log) transformation.

That is, the internal state of the universe and the spacetime we see are not the same thing.

What we call "space" and "time" may be the result of reading out a more fundamental complex state.

## The starting point is a single closure condition

The condition taken as the starting point of these two papers is very simple:

Σ zₙ² = 0

Collect many complex numbers zₙ, square them all, and require the sum to be zero.

At first glance this looks like a mere algebraic condition.

But it has one striking feature.

Rescale all the complex numbers by the same factor, and the sum of squares is still zero.

In other words, this state has no "absolute size" from the very beginning.

All it has are the relations among the complex numbers.

For this research program --- which wants to read out space, time, and matter afterwards, without ever inputting meters, seconds, or kilograms --- this property turned out to be essential.

## Perhaps inflation need not be "space expanding from the start"

In the earlier numerical experiments, giving an extremely small fluctuation to the highly symmetric complex state produced a phenomenon in which the state moved explosively into directions that had barely existed.

The magnitude of that change reaches the order of 10^30 relative to the initial fluctuation.

I have not proved that this phenomenon is the inflation of standard cosmology itself.

However, in the following respects it is very inflation-like:

- the initial state is extremely symmetric
- there is a tiny asymmetry
- that asymmetry is amplified explosively
- new directions open up enormously
- afterwards, a stable three-dimensional directional structure remains

In the present analysis, I have come to think it more natural to see this phenomenon not as "an already existing three-dimensional space expanding", but as observable directions themselves separating out from within a complex state that cannot yet be called space.

## When things settle, a homogeneous sea of complex waves remains

After the rapid separation of directions settles down, what remains is a nearly homogeneous collection of complex waves with no special center.

In these papers, this state is read as the "vacuum".

What matters here is that the vacuum is not placed inside spacetime from the start.

The order is reversed:

- first there is a complex state
- that complex state settles into a stable, homogeneous sea of waves
- and as the result of reading out that state, space and time appear

So the picture is not "a vacuum inside spacetime", but "spacetime appearing as the readout of the vacuum state".

## The complex states are not themselves space or time

This is the part of these papers that surprised me the most.

Looking at the collection of complex numbers itself, the distances and times we ordinarily use are not directly written there.

The complex states appear as space and time through a readout: the logarithmic (log) transformation.

Put simply, there are three stages:

- the internal complex state
- the readout by log
- the observed space and time

This means the fundamental state of the universe and the spacetime we observe may not be the same thing.

What we directly see may not be the fundamental state itself, but the result of reading it out.

## Then a "light-like vacuum" appears

When I constructed an explicit special solution in which countless complex waves close consistently without contradiction, a case appeared in which the spatial scales and the temporal period scales correspond exactly.

In that case, wavelength × frequency is constant.

Moreover, when the readout units of space and time are matched, every pair of scales stands in a "light-like" --- null --- relation.

This does not mean "zero closure always produces light".

Rather, it means that one particular, highly symmetric vacuum state found here can be read as a light-like, self-dual state.

This point is important.

Without placing light as something special from the start, a light-like structure can appear as the readout of a certain highly symmetric vacuum state.

## So where does matter come from?

Up to this point, the universe is a nearly homogeneous sea of complex waves.

There is no special place anywhere.

Naturally, there is no center about which one could say "there is an object here".

So the next question I considered was: what happens if a part of the homogeneous waves condenses locally?

If some of the complex waves form a cluster different from their surroundings, then for the first time it becomes possible to read out

- the strength of the condensation
- a center relative to the surroundings
- a finite extent
- a shape
- an influence on the surrounding vacuum state

This "relative center" corresponds to the center of mass of an object.

And if it has a finite extent, it is a localized entity --- neither a single point nor an infinitely spread background.

## Reading something "mass-like" without inputting mass

In ordinary physics, one writes equations of motion after first assigning a mass m to a particle.

In the present model, that order is reversed.

Instead of placing mass at the start, we read out

- how many waves participated in the condensation
- where a relative center formed
- how large the localized extent is
- how much the surrounding vacuum state is deformed

At this stage I call this not "mass itself" but a "mass-like measure".

I have not yet shown that it coincides exactly with inertial mass or gravitational mass.

But at least this much has become quite clear: a center and a finite extent, which the homogeneous vacuum did not have, are born only through localized condensation.

## Perhaps vacuum and matter were never separate things

In this way of thinking, no separate fundamental variables are prepared for vacuum and matter.

Both are made of the same complex states.

What differs is how those states are arranged, clustered, and correlated.

That is:

- spread homogeneously, the state reads as vacuum
- clustered locally, it reads as matter

In this view, matter is not "something else placed inside the vacuum".

Matter itself may be a localized condensation of the vacuum state.

## Moreover, when matter forms, the surrounding vacuum may change too

The total complex state maintains the self-consistency condition that the sum of squares is zero.

Therefore, if local condensation shifts the balance in one part, the surrounding state may rearrange itself to compensate.

In these papers I separated this into two kinds:

- a deformation in which the local contribution to the zero closure becomes biased and the surroundings compensate for it
- a deformation in which the zero closure itself is maintained while the correlations among the waves are rearranged

The conditions under which the latter truly exists are not yet known.

But if localized matter and the deformation of the surrounding vacuum are two readouts of one and the same change of state, then matter and the gravity-like background deformation may not need to be given separately.

This is an important problem to test next by numerical experiment.

## Does the light-like vacuum connect to time-like matter?

In the appendix of the second paper, I slightly generalized the light-like special state of the vacuum.

Expressing the relation between the spatial scale and the temporal period scale by a single exponent α:

- α < 1 gives spacelike
- α = 1 gives null
- α > 1 gives timelike

All three types appear within the same zero-closure system.

The highly symmetric vacuum corresponds to the self-dual point α = 1.

Then a very natural question arises:

"Could the localized condensation of matter shift the vacuum away from α = 1 and give birth to timelike states?"

This is still a hypothesis.

In the paper, I did not treat it as a result; I left it as a problem to be discriminated next by numerical experiment.

If this connection is confirmed, then

- the light-like vacuum
- localized matter
- the timelike worldlines of matter

may connect as different readouts of the same complex state.

## What we now know, and what we do not

In these two papers, at least the following has been constructed mathematically:

- relative spatial and temporal scales can be read out from the zero closure of anonymous complex numbers
- in a special, highly symmetric state, a light-like vacuum readout appears
- localized condensates can be detected anonymously in the homogeneous sea of complex waves
- the relative center of mass, finite extent, and condensation weight of a condensate can be read out
- a method can be constructed to detect the deformation of the surrounding vacuum state caused by condensation
- the light-like self-dual state extends to spacelike and timelike readouts

At the same time, what we do not yet know is equally clear:

- whether this localized condensation arises spontaneously from the zero-closed vacuum
- under what conditions a condensate remains stable over long times
- whether the "mass-like measure" read out here coincides with true inertial and gravitational mass
- whether the surrounding vacuum deformation leads to the inverse-square law of gravity at large distances
- whether localized condensation actually produces timelike states
- how far the inflation-like numerical phenomenon corresponds to the real cosmological inflation

This research is not yet a completed cosmology.

But with these results, at least one very simple thread has come into view.

## The picture of the universe I am looking at now

Put in the simplest terms:

- there is a highly symmetric complex state in which even dimensionality cannot yet be read
- a tiny fluctuation is amplified in an inflation-like way
- three-dimensional directions separate out spontaneously
- the stabilized sea of complex waves is read out as a vacuum carrying four-dimensional spacetime
- yet the internal state itself is not spacetime; it is observed as spacetime through the log transformation
- when part of the homogeneous waves condenses, a relative center and a finite extent are born
- that is read as a material entity
- and that condensation may also deform the surrounding vacuum state

In other words:

perhaps "space", "time", "vacuum", and "matter" need not be placed separately from the start.

They may all be results of reading out one more fundamental complex state in different ways.

These two papers are the first stage of investigating that possibility mathematically.
