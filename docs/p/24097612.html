The Observer's Resolution Is a Gauge: A Thought Experiment That Reads the Harmonic Order in Reverse, and the Prediction of a Theory Assembled from Known Symmetries

観測者の分解能はゲージである ― 次数を逆に読む思考実験と、既知の対称性から組み上がる理論の予言 ―

Noriaki Kihara (WF System Co., Ltd.) · ORCID 0009-0004-6753-4020 · 2026-11-10 · v1.0 · Series: Generative Structure of Dimensions

Abstract

A numerical experiment that builds quaternion pulses from integer-order harmonics on a closed circle and moves their centre phases came to a halt at a certain point: it used integer-order standing waves while treating the centre phase as a state that can be moved continuously. This paper records the chain of questions and answers that began there, in the order in which they arose, as a thought experiment in 19 steps, (T1)–(T19). The line of thought is as follows. Harmonics up to a maximum order M divide the circle into M parts, and a pulse of finite order becomes a rigid crystal. Pulses nevertheless appear to move continuously, because the wave that looks like the base wave is not the lowest-order wave but the observer's resolution, and what moves is the centre phase of a wave coarser than that resolution. The resolution is therefore not a property of the wave but a gauge of the observer. The order (winding number) and the phase are dual, and exchanging which of the two is read as the observation space exchanges the internal space and the observation space. For complex numbers this exchange is 1 ⇔ 1, its form does not change, and it cannot be seen. For a quaternion q = A e^{nθ} it becomes 1 ⇔ 3, an exchange between one closed circle and a three-component phase. What is conserved is the viewing angle; the resolution M changes. All the parts used (the duality of the circle and the integers, the finite Weyl relation, harmonic analysis on S^3, the classification of the finite subgroups of SU(2), cyclotomic fields and Niven's theorem, the McKay correspondence) are known. The prediction of this paper is that there is a map that joins these parts into one line under this reading, and that discreteness and quantumness, the continuity of three dimensions, integer magic numbers, and mass ratios that are not ratios of integers come out of it. Why there is a minimal viewing angle (Planck's constant) that does not depend on the observer is left as an open question.
