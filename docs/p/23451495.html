A Map from a Set of Complex Numbers to Spacetime (1+3): Readout by Quaternions and Partial Projection

複素数の集合から時空 (1＋3) への写像 ― 四元数による読み出しと部分投射 ―

Noriaki Kihara (WF System Co., Ltd.) · ORCID 0009-0004-6753-4020 · 2026-10-18 · v1.0 · Series: Generative Structure of Dimensions

Abstract

The author's research programme seeks to read out spacetime and particles from the relations of a set of complex numbers {zₙ} alone, without placing a background spacetime, a ruler or particle species by hand. This paper shows how to map {zₙ} to spacetime (1+3). Two complex numbers form a quaternion q = z₁ + z₂ j; as a set a quaternion is just a four-dimensional vector, but read through multiplication it splits into 1+3. The map {zₙ} (2N) → {qₙ} (N) → (1+3)(t, xyz) is built only from anonymity, self-consistency, the distinguishability axiom and the zero closures Σq = 0 and Σq² = 0. The polar form q = |q|(cos θ + n sin θ) is exactly the gnomonic (central) projection u = v/q₀ = n tan θ onto the tangent hyperplane at the north pole; the scalar part of the unconjugated square, q₀² − |v|², has signature (1, 3) and θ = 45° is the light cone. The isotropic closure (13 conditions) and the true-square closure are incompatible; the 16-cell is isotropic but has Σq² = −4. The most symmetric configuration is q = ±e^(π n/4) = ±(1 + n)/√2 with n the vertex directions of a regular polyhedron: each q lies on the light cone, q² = n and q⁸ = 1, the icosahedral case (N = 24) being the most symmetric. The number of spatial directions that N quaternions can open is min(3, N−2), saturating at three, with N = 5 the smallest for three isotropic directions. The chain is a full projection from complex numbers to quaternions and a partial projection under central projection; the norm, the sign, the equator and the global rotation escape the readout. Next tasks: the (1+3) map of localization by equal-amplitude harmonics, octonions and the (1+3)(1+3) map, and inflation-like expansion from all-to-all self-interaction.
