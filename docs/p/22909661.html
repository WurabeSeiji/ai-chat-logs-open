The Third Thought Experiment: Reading Out the State of Two Bodies a, b from the Orbit of Two Celestial Bodies a, b - Obtaining the Mass Ratio, the Relative Distance and the Relative Time from Orbit and Gravitational-Wave Readouts, with Two Values of Undetermined Meaning and the Knowledge G = c = M = 1 Alone

第三思考実験：a b 2体の天体の軌道から a b 2体の状態を読み出す

Noriaki Kihara (WF System Co., Ltd.) · ORCID 0009-0004-6753-4020 · 2026-09-23 · v1.5 · Series: Generative Structure of Dimensions

Abstract

The first and second thought experiments (10.5281/zenodo.22857952, 10.5281/zenodo.22867336) started from two nameless values a, b and derived Kepler-type orbits and an inverse-square force. The second concluded that the relative motion of two bodies yields only the single coupling mu_12, so that the mass ratio cannot be written with two values. This paper starts from a doubt about that conclusion and re-examines it along a different route. Orbits of two celestial bodies were computed with the exact Newtonian equations and with general relativity in the post-Newtonian approximation (1PN, 2PN and 2.5PN radiation reaction), and a numerical experiment tested whether the mass ratio, the relative distance and the relative time can be read out from the observed orbit. The only condition imposed on the state Psi is P1: distinguishing a state from a relation requires at least two values of undetermined meaning. What a and b are is zero knowledge for the observer. The readout side holds the knowledge G = c = M = 1 (M the total mass) and reads the data as the motion of two bodies. A distant, light observer C obtains two kinds of data: the positions of the two bodies on its celestial sphere seen along the normal of the orbital plane (observation 1), and the two polarizations of the gravitational wave (observation 2). The distance, the conversion of M into seconds, the time origin, the orientation of the celestial sphere and which body is heavier were all hidden. For all three data sets (mass ratios 1, 2 and 4) the mass ratio, the orbital shape a and b, the conversion T_M, the distance D, the relative distance r(t), the elapsed time and the difference of the proper times of the two bodies were read out. Most relative errors are 1e-4 to 1e-3, and apart from the mass ratio of D2 read from the gravitational wave alone through nu (6e-2) the largest is 4.7e-3. The noise added to the positions of observation 1 and to the amplitude of observation 2 has a standard deviation of 1/1000 of the reference quantity. Observation 1 also identified correctly which body is heavier. In a strong field, if the orbit in the two-dimensional plane can be read out, then the mass ratio, the relative distance and the relative time can be read out. Counting the observer C itself as a state extends the system to three states; this paper treats C as a test observer, and the dynamics of the three relations is the subject of the next thought experiment.
