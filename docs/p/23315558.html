The Fourteenth Thought Experiment: Why Can Different Symmetries Never Be Fully Reconciled? - Non-commensurability, Pull-in and Metastability of Integer-Selecting Symmetries, and Readout along the Direction of Evolution

第十四思考実験：なぜ異なる対称性は、完全には整合できないのか？

Noriaki Kihara (WF System Co., Ltd.) · ORCID 0009-0004-6753-4020 · 2026-10-12 · v1.0 · Series: Generative Structure of Dimensions

Abstract

This thought experiment starts from a question about why quantum mechanics explains the stability of the hydrogen atom with Planck's constant but does not ask why quantum structure exists at all. The central hypothesis: several symmetries that select integers (in space, in the order of evolution, in charge) are required simultaneously on one relational structure; they cannot be satisfied simultaneously, yet they pull each other in. This incomplete locking produces metastability and sudden change, and the observed constants appear as effective quantities read out from that history along a particular direction. The proposal of this paper is a search strategy: look for pairs of structures that appear theoretically unrelated, with no exact map or derivation between them, yet lie close to each other in number or structure; such a pair pulls in when imposed together but never locks completely. The reason for the closeness is not explained: being near but not identical is itself the clue, and a pair for which an exact correspondence (a lock) is found is removed from the candidates. The circle map (Arnold tongues), quasicrystals and the case of the fine-structure constant (the four-dimensional cell count 137 = 1 + 8 + 128, the finite-order root 23/124, and their shared integers with E8) are recorded as examples. Mathematical precedents in which the compatibility of two structures selects integers are presented: the crystallographic restriction (rotation and translation; applied to phase, only Z4 and Z6 can be cut exactly with integers, and 45 degrees is the first angle off the integer lattice, coinciding with the Clifford/T boundary), Hurwitz's theorem (multiplication and norm; dimensions 1, 2, 4, 8), and E8 (evenness and self-duality select dimension 8; integral octonions; the H4 quasicrystal; the golden-ratio mass ratio observed in CoNb2O6). Above these, the author places the zero square sum, sum x^2 = 0, as the ultimate symmetry of his theoretical system. It is not an axiom: like U^n = I it holds as a consequence of combining other symmetries (integrality and norm give finite order; multiplication and norm give the light cone identically for algebras of dimension 1, 2, 4, 8, failing at 16). The 0 is not the value of a conserved quantity: sum x^2 = R^2 becomes sum x'^2 = 0 by adding one axis iR, so the equation only defines that a conserved quantity can exist. Read by central projection, it separates observable directions from an unobservable direction; any axis may be the projection axis (anonymity); and surfaces of constant curvature share the origin. Verification scripts (standard library only, exact arithmetic) are included for the 137 cell count, integer phase lattices, E8 and the emergence of null vectors.
