The Thirteenth Thought Experiment: Why Does a Particle That Has Acquired Mass Not Dissipate and Vanish? - Coherence as a Relation, Harmonic Exchange of Localized Waves, Internal Clocks and Particle Lifetimes Considered from a Single Interaction

第十三思考実験：質量を獲得した粒子は、なぜ散逸して消滅しないのか？

Noriaki Kihara (WF System Co., Ltd.) · ORCID 0009-0004-6753-4020 · 2026-10-10 · v2.0 · Series: Generative Structure of Dimensions

Abstract

This thought experiment begins with a naive question about classifying wave-packet collapse and decoherence as separate phenomena. Using experience with laser holography as a guide, it asks whether coherence is not an absolute attribute of a single wave but depends on the partner wave, the harmonic structures of both, their phases and the readout conditions. A localized wave built from aligned high harmonics forms a sharp peak, but its spatiotemporal alignment with another localized wave is extremely restrictive. When a localized wave scatters on a monochromatic background wave, its high harmonics can be redistributed to the background. If a particle acquires a mass-like response through interaction with the background, why does it not keep losing harmonics and dissipate? Re-examining the author's earlier closed two-wave scattering experiment shows that localization is not lost in one direction but is redistributed back and forth between the two waves. The original program was re-run as a byte-identical copy (all data and figures identical to the original output): the two channels are orthogonal, so the per-collision normalization is a no-op and the update is the pure exchange operator U_R; no dissipation occurs, each wave keeps unit power while its peak height changes by a factor of about 30, and the effective harmonic orders satisfy N_A = 1 + 31 sin^2(k omega/2) with N_A + N_B = 33 exactly. If such circulation closes stably, finite periodicity U^n = I may be observed as a result, not imposed as a rule. The paper asks whether periodic closure, phase locking, quasi-stationarity and lifetimes appear as results, and whether the temporal and spatial readouts of the circulation correspond to internal clock delay and Lorentz contraction. The 'background' is treated not as a container with places, times and a number of waves, but as the anonymous part of the total state not yet distinguished by interaction readouts; the paper ends by asking whether particle species are names for conserved quantities of the interaction. Version 2.0 adds §6.2. The value R = 0.70 was originally used because a coarse reflectance sweep gave the cleanest exchange response there; later fine sweeps around 0.7 located a deep peak whose centre converged to the finite-order root R_{124,23} = cos^2(23 pi/124). With the update formula of the original program unchanged, R is replaced by this root and collisions 0-248 are recorded: the complex state itself returns to the initial state at collision 124 (distance 5.1e-15), with complete interchange at collision 62. Because R is an input, this period is a consequence of the input value, not evidence that periodic closure arises from the interaction. Two runs reproduced all output files bit for bit. Changes from v1.0 are listed in the revision history at the end of the paper. This is a thought experiment that formulates verification tasks arising from the reinterpretation of existing numerical experiments; it does not complete derivations of mass, the Lorentz metric, quantum energy levels or real particle lifetimes. Main text in Japanese with an English translation; the verification package of the R = 0.70 re-run is included as a zip archive, and that of the exact-root run is in the GitHub repository.
