A Catalogue of Symmetries and Magic Numbers for Reading Out Spacetime from a State Space: The Answer-Check Standard of a Research Programme Taking Anonymity and Self-Consistency as Axioms

状態空間から時空を読み出すための対称性とマジックナンバーの目録 ― 無名性と自己無撞着を公理とする研究計画の答え合わせの基準 ―

Noriaki Kihara (WF System Co., Ltd.) · ORCID 0009-0004-6753-4020 · 2026-10-18 · v1.0 · Series: Generative Structure of Dimensions

Abstract

This paper fixes the answer-check standard of a research programme that seeks to read out time, space, mass, charge and interactions from the relations of a set of complex numbers {zₙ} alone, without placing a background spacetime, a ruler or particle species by hand. It presents no new derivation; it catalogues the symmetries the programme must satisfy and the dimensionless numbers (magic numbers) attached to them, with a status (established, theory, candidate, open) and a provenance for every entry. The zeroth axiom is anonymity and the first is self-consistency; a distinguishability axiom derived from anonymity states that the readout cannot tell which layer a value belongs to, since z′ = z² is also a complex number. The single closure definition is the linear zero closure Σzₙ = 0, and the true-square closure Σzₙ² = Σz′ₙ = 0 is the same definition read at the squared layer; Uⁿ = I is a theorem. Big-picture symmetries, conjugacy and duality, the projection symmetry, a candidate axiom that essential symmetries carry dimensionless magic numbers rather than Noether conserved quantities, the requirements of the Standard Model, general relativity and E₈, and locking symmetries of complex systems are listed in one format; magic numbers are split into integer, coupling-convention, irrational and derived systems. Two checks are included: only real orthogonal maps preserve both the true square and the norm, and the 24 Hurwitz units split 8 + 8 + 8 and are cycled by triality q ↦ a q a, preserving the 240 roots of E₈. All 156 published papers are mapped to the rows; the axiom numbering is reset, an earlier lock-type claim on α is revised, and numerical disagreements are kept as open problems with several interpretations. The root difficulty of the state-space-to-spacetime map is placed in the dependence of what is readable on the projection method; the map must be at least a partial projection.
