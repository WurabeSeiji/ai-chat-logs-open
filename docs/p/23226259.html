Abstract

The fine-structure constant α was used as a plain input value in the Eleventh Thought Experiment. This note asks where α comes from. In the Bohr ground state of hydrogen α = v/c. The Lorentz factor γ = 1/√(1−α²) is only a component ratio of the light-cone equation x²+y²+z²+(it)²=0 with one proper-time axis τ added, r²+τ²+(it)²=0: a slower-than-light worldline lies on the light cone once one axis is added. The same right triangle is the central projection ℓ² = |x|² + R_c², with R_c = √(R²+ΣQᵢ²) the curvature radius in the projection direction and t ↔ ℓ. Extending the light cone to eight unnamed directions (x,y,z,t,R,Q₁,Q₂,Q₃), an observer reading a subspace sees the hidden directions only as a sum of squares; transformations G with P(U)=P(GU) are observational symmetries, so symmetry is read as indistinguishability. An observer reading only t sees an O(7)-type symmetry, and a 3+2 split of the five hidden directions gives S(U(3)×U(2)) ≅ (SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1))/ℤ₆, the same structure as the SU(5) 3+2 split. This is a conceptual note without computation; the eight dimensions, signature and 3+2 split are put in by hand.
