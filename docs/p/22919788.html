The Fourth Thought Experiment: From a Fixed Action S to a State-Dependent Action S_n - Can the Relativistic Gravitational Orbits Computed Externally in the Third Thought Experiment Be Regenerated from the Sequential Interaction X_{n+1} = S_n X_n?

第四思考実験：固定作用 S から状態依存作用 S_n へ

Noriaki Kihara (WF System Co., Ltd.) · ORCID 0009-0004-6753-4020 · 2026-09-23 · v1.0 · Series: Generative Structure of Dimensions

Abstract

This paper starts from an inconsistency left between the second and the third thought experiments. The second thought experiment (10.5281/zenodo.22867336) applied a fixed action S repeatedly to an internal state X_n made of two values of undetermined meaning, and generated inverse-square orbits from a product readout and an area clock. The third thought experiment (10.5281/zenodo.22909660) reversed the direction: two-body orbits were computed externally with known Newtonian mechanics and the post-Newtonian approximation, and it was tested whether the mass ratio, the relative distance and the relative time could be read out from the observational data. That confirmed the readout capability, but the generation of the orbit itself remained in the known equations of gravity. This paper therefore asks whether the relativistic orbits given from outside in the third thought experiment can again be generated from the sequential interaction of internal states. Keeping the state representation and the readout of this series, a fixed action S would require processing-dependent information to be brought in from outside the action in order to represent, at the same time, the periastron advance that depends on the orbital position and the change of the orbital elements by radiation reaction. As the minimal generalization, the action itself is made a function of the present relational state: X_{n+1} = S_n X_n with S_n = S(R_n) and det S_n = 1. Here n is a processing step, not a physical time; the physical time t is read out separately from the state sequence. The conservative part uses the Darwin parameterization of bound Schwarzschild orbits, whose angular progression is built as a recurrence to arbitrary order (N = 12 in the experiments). The same 2.5PN radiation reaction as in the third thought experiment is added as a local update of the orbital elements p_n and e_n. For the first strong-field condition, adding the radiation reaction improved the normalized radial RMS error against the reference PN orbit from 2.51 per cent to 1.95 per cent. The same generating rule for S_n was then applied, without change, to nine conditions with p = 24.3, 60, 120 and e = 0.20, 0.45, 0.55. The normalized radial RMS error over the nine cases has median 0.508 per cent, mean 0.832 per cent and maximum 2.864 per cent, and the agreement improves systematically in weaker fields. Fixing p = 60 and e = 0.45 and comparing mass ratios m_A/m_B = 1, 2, 4, 10, the normalized radial RMS error stays between 0.508 and 0.438 per cent, and the periastron shrinkage by radiation reaction is about 92 per cent of the PN reference in all conditions. In every experiment |det S_n - 1| stayed within floating-point precision. This paper does not derive general relativity from S_n, nor does it claim state-dependent discrete updates as a new mathematical formalism. What is shown is the feasibility and the transferability: by the minimal extension from a fixed action S to a state-dependent action S_n, one and the same generating rule can represent, as internal state updates, a family of gravitational orbits with precession, dissipation, different orbital shapes and different mass ratios.
