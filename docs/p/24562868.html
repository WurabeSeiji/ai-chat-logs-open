The Coin and Cup Thought Experiment ― Reverse-Deriving the Requirement Specification of a System from the Conditions under Which an Action Holds ―

コインとコップの思考実験 ― 作用の成立条件から、系の要求仕様を逆算する ―

Noriaki Kihara (WF System Co., Ltd.) · ORCID 0009-0004-6753-4020 · 2026-11-26 · v1.0 · Series: Generative Structure of Dimensions

Abstract

An article on note by Professor Masahiro Hotta builds the formalism of linear algebra used in quantum mechanics from a classical stochastic process: a coin inside a cup. Reading this article, I became interested in something apart from its subject, namely the setting of the cup and the coin itself. The knowledge before the cup is lifted is not zero; it is nothing at all (null). Then how can one distinguish that it is not null? This paper is a record, in the order in which it was carried out, of the thought experiment that began from this question.The line of argument is as follows. To say that something is not null, there is no way but to decide a resolution. A resolution is not an absolute value but a ratio to the whole. If the span from putting the cup down to lifting it, or the diameter of the cup, is read as a half-wavelength (a window), the relation between the cup and the coin can be written as a relation between two waves. The condition for observation to hold is not that the two waves are coherent, but that the cross term (the product of the two waves, the AND of information) can be read. Three assumptions are placed. The action can be defined uniquely and read uniquely. The window is stationary. The system has no special direction and no origin. From these three it is required that the system be finite, closed, without ends, and without dents or vertices. This is because, in a flat system with ends, the cup cannot be moved while remaining the same cup. No background time is placed in this system. Since there is no time, there are no traveling waves, and only standing waves exist. That the window is stationary does not mean that it is constant with respect to an outside clock. It means that the partner that maintains the window (a wave shifted by a quarter wavelength) exists on the system itself. From this requirement, the system is narrowed down to the circle S¹ or the three-sphere S³.The thought experiment came to a standstill once. If both the coin and the cup are drawn as convex peaks, the cross term takes the same value when the two are interchanged, and the asymmetric relation "the cup hides the coin" does not come out. The next day, when the cup was replaced by a well-shaped waveform (amplitude 0 at the place it covers, constant elsewhere), the cross term alone gave: hidden when overlaid, partly visible when the rim falls on the coin, visible when shifted away. At this point the question was replaced, from whether the coin "is there or is absent" to whether the coin "is visible or not visible". I gave up distinguishing "not visible" from "absent". Since null cannot be read directly, both absent (null) and not visible are read as: the amplitude of the cross term is at or below the resolution of amplitude. What is known when the cup is put over the coin is only that the coin has become not visible; whether there is a coin inside the cup is not known. There was no need to add a background, an observer, or an order of operations. That the radius R of the circle is placed by hand in the figures is also not a problem, because the residents on the circle cannot read R.Of these, the calculations of the waveforms and the cross term were checked with the attached programs. The mathematics used (Fourier series, the Fejér weights, the theorem on vector fields on the two-sphere, the classification of parallelizable spheres) is known. Reading an act as a wave, taking the readability of the cross term as the condition for observation, the requirement on the shape of the system from the three assumptions, and in particular the narrowing down to S¹ or S³, are hypotheses, and verification is required.This paper is a hypothesis paper and an interpretation paper. It contains no new theorem. What this paper states is that, when the conditions for the familiar act of putting the cup down and lifting it to hold are traced backward, the requirement specification of the system that carries that act is determined this far.
