[Is the electron a particle, or a wave? —— a double-slit thought experiment]
See the comment for details:

The electron is a particle and yet also a wave. The relation that gives it a wavelength is de Broglie's, λ = h/p, where the famous Planck constant h makes its appearance. The 1927 Davisson–Germer experiment measured, for real, that the electron carries a wavelength.

I tried to "reproduce" this, as a thought experiment, in the simplest classical wave model of a double slit.

What is interesting: even if we treat the electron as a lump of wave packed with structure finer than its own wavelength (a localized sum of odd harmonics), it still survives as sharp interference —— provided an "alignment" condition is met. And the fringe spacing, no matter how many harmonics you add, is set by the fundamental wavelength λ alone.

From h = pλ, Planck's constant does come back too; but honestly, that is a self-consistency (a simulated reproduction) because we put λ = h/p in at the start — not a re-measurement of h. The genuinely interesting point is the other one: that even with finer structure packed in, the interference survives at the de Broglie wavelength scale.
