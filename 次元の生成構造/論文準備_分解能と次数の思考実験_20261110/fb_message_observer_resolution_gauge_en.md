Why does a wave that should not move appear to move? The observer's resolution and the extra dimension, out of a numerical experiment that got stuck

Details in the comments

In a theory with no background time, a wave is written as a standing wave without time. I had been moving the peak of a localised wave built from its harmonics continuously, with no basis. This paper examines that hidden assumption. It is a hypothesis paper and proves nothing
Only integer-order harmonics stand on a closed string. Harmonics up to order M divide the circle into M parts, and a pulse of finite order becomes a rigid crystal with no room to shift its peak
Yet pulses appear to move continuously. The mistake was to read the base wave as the wave of order 1
What looks like the base wave is the observer's resolution, and the orders of the wave go on without end. A pulse shape is an infinitely sharp pulse smoothed at the resolution. The formula of the experiment had that form from the start
What moves is the peak of a wave coarser than the resolution, and that position too is discretised by the graduation of the resolution
So the resolution is not a property of the wave but a gauge of the observer. What stays unchanged when the resolution changes is the viewing angle; the viewing angle is the conserved quantity
The winding number (order) and the phase are dual, and mathematics does not decide which is placed on the side of observation. Exchanging them exchanges the internal space and the observation space
For complex numbers this exchange is 1 and 1 and cannot be seen. For a quaternion A e^{nθ} it is an exchange of 1 and 3: one closed circle and a three-component phase
The observable three-dimensional space is on the side that has been called the extra dimension. This reading points the opposite way from Kaluza–Klein
All the parts used (the duality of the circle and the integers, the finite Weyl relation, the finite subgroups of SU(2), Niven's theorem, the McKay correspondence) are known. The prediction is that only the connecting map is missing. Why there is a minimal viewing angle (Planck's constant) is left open
