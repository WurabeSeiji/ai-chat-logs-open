Do the symmetries of physics really have to be given from the start? — Reverse-engineering spacetime, curvature, quantization, and even the Standard Model gauge group from just four closure conditions (new paper published)

Details in the comments:

There is something a little strange about physics. The deeper the theory goes, the more symmetries it accumulates: Lorentz symmetry, gauge symmetry, SU(3), SU(2), U(1), cyclic symmetry, permutation symmetry, symplectic structure, conformal structure. Every one of them is an important structure supporting modern physics. But I decided, for once, to think in the opposite direction — do these really have to be posited from the start, each as a separate principle? The question investigated in this paper is an extreme one: if we posit only a handful of closure conditions, how much of the symmetry that modern theoretical physics demands appears afterwards, automatically?

There are only four axioms. First, zero closure: the sum of complex squares vanishes, Σxn²=0. Second, finite recurrence: applying the action N times returns everything exactly, U^N=I. Third, simplex consistency: all pairwise distances among N vertices close into a consistent simplex. Fourth, self-consistency: feeding the generated state back into the generating rule returns the state itself, X=F(X). The only essential quantity specified from outside is a discrete integer N; within the derived range there is not a single continuous tuning parameter.

What comes out of these four? First, the negative signature of spacetime. Reading an unobservable direction with an imaginary tag, the Lorentz-type sign emerges from nothing more than complex multiplication: (it)²=−t². No Minkowski metric needs to be imposed from outside. By the same mechanism, the coordinate value of an invisible complex direction iR is read from the three visible directions as a curvature radius: x²+y²+z²=R² — the familiar sphere equation turns out to be the zero closure with one component simply not being read by the observer. The negative sign of time and the origin of curvature are folded into one and the same equation. This is the foundation of the whole reorganization.

From the finite recurrence U^N=I come cyclic groups, phase discretization, cyclotomic structure, and the Born-type squared weights cos²(πm/N), sin²(πm/N) found in earlier work. Probability is not put into the axioms; it appears as the squared readout of a finitely recurring phase structure. From simplex consistency, coordinate-free distance geometry and the chain complex ∂²=0 — the entry to the formal structure gauge geometry requires — stand up simultaneously.

And here is the part that surprised me most. Reading the zero closure through six complex registers gives six complex degrees of freedom with one complex constraint — exactly five complex dimensions remain. Simple dimension counting. When these five complex degrees of freedom enter a sector preserving a Hermitian 3⊕2 decomposition, and the redundant unobservable global phase is removed, known group theory produces S(U(3)×U(2))≅[SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)]/Z6 — the same form as the faithful global gauge group of the Standard Model. And the road is not unique. Reading the internal quantity more finely as Q²=Q1²+Q2²+Q3² opens an internal-triplet route to SU(3); reading (R,Q1,Q2,Q3) as four components connects to SU(4)⊃SU(3)×U(1); another readout on the spacetime side yields Spin(4)≅SU(2)×SU(2) — so even a route toward the Pati–Salam-type unified structure comes into view as just another reading of the same zero closure.

Let me head off the obvious objection: if there are multiple readings, can't you get anything you want? The answer is the opposite — this model is quite cramped. The fundamental components carry no names, but an admissible structure must simultaneously pass all the independent strong constraints: zero closure, finite recurrence, simplex consistency, harmonic structure, and self-consistency. Symmetry is not something chosen from outside; it is what the observer reads as the automorphisms of the structures that survived the strong constraints. This inversion of order is the real subject of the paper. Perhaps physics has so many symmetries not because there is much freedom, but because there is extremely little — almost no structures capable of closing into existence remain, and that is why what remains looks beautiful.

Since these are strong claims, the boundaries are stated explicitly. I am not claiming to have completely derived the Standard Model from the first axiom alone. What comes out rigorously is the five complex degrees of freedom; why the 3⊕2 decomposition is selected self-consistently is registered as an open problem, as are general Riemannian dynamics, local gauge connections, and chirality, hypercharge, and anomaly cancellation. The paper grades every structure line by line in a derivation map with four statuses: rigorous, conditionally rigorous, numerically confirmed in the self-papers, and not yet derived. What used to look like separate grand problems has been compressed into a small number of concrete, not-yet-derived spots — that is the step forward this time.
