# Antimatter Came Out Without Being Put In — How the Grammar of Matter Emerged from a Single Equation That Knows Nothing

Noriaki Kihara  
August 5, 2026

In the previous articles, I reported that our wave model expands rapidly on its own (inflation), gives birth to three directions, and then stops. We also learned that only a clear, coherent wave on the verge of tipping over can ignite that rapid expansion.

So the big remaining question is this:

**How does matter arise from that sea of light-like waves?**

The new paper takes on this question directly.

Let me give away part of the conclusion first.

Matter-like structure is never born without a seed; with a seed, it multiplies itself at a rate proportional to the square of the seed fraction; when it grows too much, it stops at about one half; and — **it always brings along a partner corresponding to antimatter, without ever being asked to.**

Even though antimatter was never put in anywhere.

## An honest preface first

This paper contains an explicit assumption.

The model so far had exactly one rule for wave-wave collisions (one interaction). But that rule never changed the combination of notes. Waves collide, their mixture changes, yet no new note is ever created. In the language of physics, the collision is elastic.

To create new notes — the components corresponding to matter — a new collision rule that changes the combination of notes has to be added.

So we posited that rule as an assumption. We did not derive it. It is a hypothesis: let us try placing it this way. The paper itself separates, from the very beginning, what is assumed, what follows mathematically from the assumption, and what is measured in experiments.

It is not a paper that proves the rule correct. It is a paper that measures, without cheating, what happens if you assume it.

## But the assumption was placed under a strict rule of law

We did not place it arbitrarily. We imposed the discipline that runs through the entire model:

**The collision rule may look only at the shapes of the two waves. It may know nothing else.**

That means the rule contains none of the following branches:

- this one is a boson, so do this
- this one is a fermion, so do that
- this one is a seed, so treat it specially
- let us create the antimatter at this position

In programming terms, there are zero IF statements. Even the strength of the collision is not an externally supplied constant: it is read off from the wave shapes themselves (the reflection rate that is read out is used directly as the strength, so not a single new constant is introduced).

Therefore, all the words that appear below — boson, fermion, seed, partner — are names assigned afterwards by looking at the output. The rule itself does not know what it is creating.

This is the most important point of the paper. A single equation that knows nothing nonetheless obeys a selection rule, ignites, saturates, and brings along a partner. The differentiation of roles happens on the output side, not the input side.

## The axiom narrowed the form down to one

The assumed rule still had freedom left in its form. There was a choice in how to combine its two terms.

But when we demand that the axiom at the foundation of this model — the closure condition that the squares of all waves sum to zero — be preserved before and after the collision, the combination gets pinned down uniquely.

Within the posited class, the axiom selects the form for you. This is the part that follows mathematically from the assumption.

## Just before finishing, something surprising was discovered

During the final checks of the paper, something unexpected became clear.

The uniquely determined rule looks like a complicated nonlinear expression. But when you transform the expression, its true identity turns out to be astonishingly simple.

**At each point, it merely rotates the two waves by a tiny angle.**

The rotation angle is read off from the mismatch (the relative phase) of the two waves at that location. And a rotation destroys nothing. The closure condition and the energy-like quantity are both preserved perfectly at every point. We verified that nothing leaks, down to the precision limit of the computer (fourteen decimal places).

Then where do the new notes come from?

Because the rotation angle differs from place to place.

Rotate everything by the same angle and nothing changes. Rotate each place by a different angle, and the makeup of the notes changes while everything stays perfectly conserved.

**Generation is not creation. It is redistribution under perfect conservation.**

I think this is the most beautiful finding in the paper.

## Experiment 1 — Without a seed, nothing is born

The first experiment is a seedless sea.

We took a sea made only of the boson-like components (the even-numbered notes) and stirred it with strong collisions two hundred times.

The fermion-like components (the odd-numbered notes) stayed exactly zero to the very end. Down to the limit of measurement precision, not a single one was ever born.

How can an equation without IF statements obey a strict prohibition? The reason is arithmetic. Add and subtract even numbers and you only ever get even numbers. This arithmetic, preserved by the multiplications inside the equation, is itself the selection rule.

So in this model, **there is no spontaneous generation of matter. Generation always begins by being induced by a small seed.** Interestingly, textbook physics knows the same thing: a theory of purely classical waves has no spontaneous emission.

## Experiment 2 — The seed multiplies itself by its own square

Next we mixed a tiny seed (odd-numbered components) into the sea, varying the seed fraction over a range of ten thousand to one.

A clean law emerged.

**The growth rate is proportional to the square of the seed fraction.**

The proportionality constant stayed fixed to 0.4 percent while the seed varied over four orders of magnitude.

The more seed there is, the faster the seed itself multiplies. Fire calling fire — an autocatalytic structure. Conversely, a sea with a small seed fraction does not ignite for astronomical times. This is a candidate structure for why a sea of light does not instantly fill up with matter.

Incidentally, we searched for prior work corresponding to this square law and found none. In seeded optical amplification, the pump sets the growth rate and the seed only sets the starting point. A law in which the rate itself is set by the square of the seed is, as far as we searched, new. If you know of a prior example, please let us know.

## Experiment 3 — Not everything becomes matter. It stops at about one half

Once the seed catches fire, does the whole sea turn into fermions?

It did not.

Running three thousand collisions, the fraction of fermion-like components sprinted up to sixty percent, then oscillated back and forth and **settled statistically at 0.469.**

(Insert Figure 2 here: `次元の生成構造/万能非弾性写像_managed_v1/fig2_fate_v1.png`)

![Figure 2](note_fig2_fate_v1.png)

Figure 2. The time course of the fermion fraction. After the steep rise there is no runaway; it settles into a statistical equilibrium. The dotted line is 0.494, the fraction of seats classified as fermionic. They nearly coincide.

The value 0.469 nearly matches a simple count of seats: about half of all the note-seats are classified as fermionic (0.494). The rotation can also run backwards — this collision is a reversible elementary process — so the system settles where forward and backward balance, a place like thermal equilibrium.

## Experiment 4 — And then the partner appeared

The last experiment is the one that surprised me most.

We put a narrow-band pump and a single seed into the sea and zoomed in on the very beginning of the collisions (before things get stirred up).

Beforehand, we wrote down predictions on paper. If this collision rule creates pairs, the new components should appear only at the specific seats where the addition balances — the partner seats. Nothing should appear in the other 485 empty seats.

The results:

- the new components appeared only at the predicted partner seats
- all 485 unpredicted empty seats stayed at zero to measurement precision (ten to the minus twenty-seventh power of the partner seats)
- the partner was born strongly correlated with the seed (correlation strength 0.83 — and even the winding-number offset at which the correlation appears matched the machine calculation made in advance)
- the partner seats rose from exact zero, simultaneously with the other components

Let me repeat: nothing corresponding to antimatter was put into this model. Not in the initial conditions, not in the collision rule.

And yet, generation always brought along the partner that balances the books.

From this, one view emerges.

**Perhaps an antiparticle is not a second species that exists from the start, but the role of a counterpart that the event of generation requires in order to close its ledger.**

Look at a single wave alone and ask whether it is a particle or an antiparticle, and the question does not resolve. The distinction is determined as a mutual relation within the event of being born — inside this model, that is how it works.

## The mistakes are written down too

As is the rule of this series, the full record of mistakes is in the paper. This time there were six.

The rule we placed first violated the conservation law, and investigating how it broke became, in reverse, the discovery of the theorem that fixes the form uniquely. We built the ledger for measuring the partner correlation by hand, got it wrong, and when we let the machine derive it, it matched the measurement exactly. A quirk of the numerical integrator had us misreading the proportionality constant by ten percent, and later it turned out no integration was needed at all (the rule is exactly solvable) — in the end everything became more accurate by many orders of magnitude.

Failures hidden become debt; failures recorded become assets.

## The same equation was already inside optical fibers

After finishing the paper, we re-surveyed prior work and found something delightful.

An equation of the same form as the collision rule we had narrowed down from the axiom already exists — in the equations describing how light propagates in optical fibers. The combination of terms by which two polarizations mix has the same algebraic form.

But there is a decisive difference. In an optical fiber, the coefficients of the terms are set by the properties of the material, the glass. In our model, they are set by the axiom of closure.

Same form, different reason. This is encouraging evidence that our assumption is not an armchair construction. The phenomenon of seed and partner being born in pairs is itself also known in optics, as seeded four-wave mixing. What is new in our model is that all of it comes out of nothing but a know-nothing equation and an axiom, not out of the properties of a material.

## Summary

- We posited the generation rule of matter-like structure as an assumption — under the discipline that it sees only wave shapes and contains no IF statements
- The axiom of closure narrowed the form to exactly one within the posited class
- The rule's true identity was a perfectly conserving rotation at each point. Generation is redistribution under perfect conservation
- Without a seed, nothing is born (the prohibition comes from the arithmetic of the equation)
- The seed multiplies itself by its own square (a novelty candidate; information on prior examples is welcome)
- No runaway: it stops statistically at about one half, as the count of seats dictates
- And the partner corresponding to antimatter was always born simultaneously, unbidden, in the form that closes the ledger

Perhaps the species of a particle is not an input to the interaction, but a classification read off afterwards from the history of interactions.

Which comes first, attributes or relations? This model consistently answers: relations.
