# In this model, attraction and repulsion came out of one and the same rule

-- Keeping two numbers, deriving a Coulomb-type force: the second thought experiment

Noriaki Kihara

## This continues the previous article

In the previous article I wrote about how elliptic and hyperbolic orbits appear from nothing but two nameless numbers and the simplest possible rule of interaction.

Something was missing at that stage, though. The centre of force sat at the centre of the ellipse. When a planet goes around the sun, the sun is not at the centre of the ellipse but at a focus. The form of the central force was different too: it pulled in proportion to the distance. Real gravity and real electric forces fall off as the inverse square of the distance. The inverse-square force did not appear last time.

This time the question is the following. Can a Coulomb-type inverse-square force be written with one state and one relation alone? May the interaction stay linear?

The answer first: it may stay linear. The inverse-square force comes not from the side of the rule, but from the side of how things are read out and how the clock is built.

## Only two things were added

The rule itself is exactly as before; nothing in it was changed. What was newly added is the following two things.

First: position, too, cannot be obtained without being read out. One of the principles last time was that a state alone is unreadable. This time the same principle is applied to position as well. That is, the position of a point is not a coordinate given in advance; it becomes readable only as a product of two configurations.

Second: the clock, too, is fixed from the readout. Instead of importing a scale of time from somewhere, it is fixed on the readout plane itself.

A metric, an inner product, complex numbers, an externally given time coordinate, a force law, mass and charge are not posited as assumptions at the starting point.

## The ways of reading out come down to three

Reading out position does not mean the way of reading is free.

The two numbers have no names, so swapping them gives the same thing. This follows from the principle of the previous article. To that, this time, one design assumption is added: that a second reflection exists. The orientation of the reflection may be chosen freely, and the conclusions do not depend on which orientation is chosen, but the existence of a second reflection is itself the assumption made here.

Taking the non-identity elements of the combinations these two generate gives exactly three. There are exactly three independent readouts, and every other readout is a combination of those three.

Call the three readouts x, y and r. Then, without any calculation, the following relation holds.

x squared + y squared = r squared

This is the Pythagorean theorem. It was not posited as an assumption. It holds from the start among the three readouts.

What is interesting here is that there is no need to choose which one to call the distance. The only readout that is unchanged under both of these two operations is r. The other two change sign under them. So r is the distance and the other two are the position. It was not decided; there was no other way for it to come out.

## The clock is fixed by the area swept

Next comes the clock.

Last time I wrote that the only readable quantity is an area. The same is used here. On the readout plane, the scale of the clock is fixed so that the area swept per unit of physical time is constant.

The time per step is then fixed, up to the overall unit of time. The form it takes is that it is proportional to the inner product of two successive configurations. It is not exactly the same as the distance itself.

If the step size is made finer and the continuum limit is taken, however, the tick of physical time becomes proportional to the distance. For the same amount of internal progress, more physical time corresponds where the distance is larger. Seen the other way round, the number of interactions per unit of physical time is inversely proportional to the distance, and is larger where things are closer.

Time has thus become a quantity built from the readout rather than a coordinate given from outside. It is not an independent degree of freedom.

While the system stays discrete, however, the tick of the clock can become negative, and the range in which it can serve as a monotone physical clock is subject to a condition. This point failed one of the numerical checks, and the failure is recorded as it happened in the paper.

That the area is constant is Kepler's second law itself, the law that a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times. Here it was not discovered as a law; it was posited as the definition of the clock.

## The inverse-square force appears

Once position and the clock are fixed in this way, the motion of the readout point is determined. The calculation gives an acceleration along the line joining the origin and the point, with a magnitude inversely proportional to the square of the distance. If the coupling is positive it is an attraction towards the origin; if negative, a repulsion away from it. This is the inverse-square force.

Moreover the centre of force sits at the focus of the orbit. Last time it was at the centre of the ellipse. Passing through the readout moves the centre to the focus.

The shape of the orbit is a conic: an ellipse, a parabola or a hyperbola. And the equation of that orbit turned out to be the conserved quantity from the previous paper itself. Last time it was merely a formula that did not change from step to step. This time, on the readout plane, it is the equation of a conic with the origin as focus. This is Kepler's first law.

One and the same quantity was a conserved quantity last time and the shape of the orbit this time. This was the most surprising point of this work.

## Attraction and repulsion come out of the same rule

Here is the main subject of this article.

Electric forces come in two kinds: attracting and repelling. Usually this is explained by giving charges a plus or minus sign from outside.

This model has no charge. Even so, both attraction and repulsion appear, and they appear from within one and the same rule.

A hyperbolic-type rule has two special directions. Those two cut the plane into four sectors. Which sector the point is in decides whether the force is attractive or repulsive. If it is in a sector whose opening angle between the two directions is larger than a right angle it is an attraction; if smaller, a repulsion.

There is also the case where the two special directions are exactly orthogonal. Then the coupling vanishes for every orbit of that rule, and the readout point moves in a straight line at constant speed. This is the case that corresponds to neutrality. A relation carrying no charge had a place in the model from the start, without any special adjustment.

Attraction and repulsion come out as a difference of sectors, and neutrality as the case where the two special directions are orthogonal. None of them requires the sign of a charge to be given from outside, and that is one of the conclusions of this work.

## What did not come out is recorded too

Writing only about what worked would not be a record of research. Here is what did not come out.

It turned out that the signed coupling coefficient and the angular momentum are proportional within one and the same rule. It is a relation that holds exactly as a formula.

But this also means trouble. For an orbit with zero angular momentum -- a collision orbit falling straight into the centre -- the coupling vanishes at the same time. In other words, an orbit that falls into the centre while a force is acting cannot be written in this model.

The map and the clock being used are known in mathematics as the Levi-Civita regularization, which is precisely a tool for handling collisions. The tool has the same form here, yet the very collisions it is meant to handle lie outside the model.

The first draft wrote this as saying that the singularity of a collision is not on the side of the law. That was wrong. It was found in review and re-derivation, and has been rewritten.

I record it rather than hide it. The design document says that finding something to be infeasible is also a result. Since it is now clear where this system breaks, it is also clear what has to be loosened in the next thought experiment.

## What two numbers cannot write

There is one more thing that cannot be written: a map.

When only two points are placed in the plane, the shape freedom left after removing translation, rotation and scaling is zero. Between two points, the notion of direction does not exist in principle. Direction acquires meaning only when a third point arrives.

The mass ratio and the charge ratio cannot be written either. All that can be read from the relative motion of two bodies is a single number in which mass and charge are mixed together, and the individual ratios cannot be recovered from it. The same holds in standard physics: measuring a mass ratio requires further information that allows two accelerations to be compared against one and the same reference.

That an observed force is nonlinear does not mean that the interaction underneath it is nonlinear. A linear rule, plus a quadratic readout, plus a clock built from the readout, gives an inverse-square force. I think that single line is the largest consequence of this work.

## How it was verified

The same way as last time.

Before running the numerical checks, sixteen conditions of the form "if this happens, it fails" were declared in advance. One of them actually failed. The cause was not an error in a theorem but an insufficiency in how the condition had been written. The result was left in the paper without rewriting, together with an account of why it happened.

All the formulas in the appendix were checked by symbolic computation. There are a hundred and ten lines, and all of them came out zero. The verdicts were run in two environments differing in both operating system and numerical library, and they agreed completely.

A separate line of AI was used for review, over three rounds. Each round found an error. There was a clear mistake, that the eccentricity is fixed by the number of the law alone, and the reviewing side and the re-deriving side independently arrived at the same counterexample.

After the first release, v1.0, six internal inconsistencies were found and corrected in v1.1. A final audit just before releasing v1.1 corrected five more. The largest of these was in the cross-check against Kepler's third law, where one revolution in the original plane and one revolution in the readout plane had been confused and the coefficient was off by a factor of two. The verification program was right; only the text of the paper was off. The full history of the corrections is kept in the work record of the paper.

## What was learned

Keeping the rule linear and fixing position and the clock on the readout side: that alone produced an inverse-square force with the origin as focus, together with attraction, repulsion and neutrality. The conserved quantity of the previous paper turned out to be the equation of the orbit itself.

And it became clear that a collision with a nonvanishing coupling cannot be written, and that the map, the mass ratio and the charge ratio cannot be written.

Three bodies come next. When there are three relations, can the clock be made common? Do the map and the charge become readable at the same time? That is the next branching point.
