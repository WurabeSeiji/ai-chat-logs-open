# The inflation happened without a seed — and the directions stopped at three

Noriaki Kihara  
2026-07-27

The universe, right after its birth, expanded by many orders of magnitude in an instant.

Modern cosmology has a powerful idea for this, called inflation theory.

And even the latest versions of inflation theory assume, at the very start of that expansion, the existence of a quantum fluctuation called zero-point vibration.

Not a perfectly quiet nothing, but a tiny, tiny fluctuation that comes first and pulls the trigger.

The existence of the trigger is an assumption.

As it turns out, my model had the same thing.

## The story so far

In this series, I have been studying a system that places no background space from the start and begins only from the relations among waves.

In the previous article, "Why does the wave stop splitting?", I reported the following.

From a seed too small to measure, a single wave amplifies exponentially and splits on its own — much like the inflation of the early universe.

Then the amplification stops at a certain point, and at that moment, new directions that had been empty until then stand up with finite magnitude.

But there was something in that experiment I should confess honestly.

**I, too, had planted a seed corresponding to the fluctuation — by hand.**

A minuscule seed, appearing only at the fifteenth decimal place.

Just as inflation theory assumes zero-point vibration, I had assumed the first fluctuation and put it in.

Then an obvious suspicion arises.

Did the rapid expansion happen only because the seed was planted?

Were the new directions born only because the seed taught them where to point?

In this paper, I settled that suspicion.

## I removed every seed

What I did was simple.

I deleted the line of the program that inserts the seed.

The system was given only the relations among waves and the condition of being closed. Zero assumptions about fluctuations.

What happened?

**The inflation-like rapid expansion still happened.**

And it happened at every system size I examined: five bodies, forty bodies, three hundred bodies.

What is even more surprising is the timing.

Comparing the arrival time of the rapid expansion with and without the seed —

- in the five-body system, the difference was 1 step
- in the forty-body system, the difference was zero
- in the three-hundred-body system, the difference was 5 steps

out of experiments running thousands of steps.

With or without the seed, the system expanded rapidly in the same way, at almost the same time.

So the seed was not the trigger.

**In this system, inflation starts on its own, without assuming any fluctuation.**

## The true face of the run-up period — it looked still, but it never rested

Watching the seedless system while recording every single step, I found something remarkable.

Before the rapid expansion there is a run-up period where the graph looks completely flat.

For about a thousand steps, nothing seems to happen.

But change the scale to logarithmic, and the landscape transforms.

**During the period that looked flat, the system was growing without a single pause.**

Counting every step-by-step change: 1,129 increases, zero decreases, zero ties.

It is the same as savings growing by compound interest, without missing a single day.

The growth rate is about 5 percent per step. Every 46 steps, it multiplies by ten.

Compound interest is invisible at first. Then, past a certain day, it suddenly looks explosive.

What looked like a "sudden onset" of inflation was not sudden at all.

**The compound growth had been running from the very beginning — it simply reached a visible size.**

Figure 2. The growth of the seedless system on a logarithmic scale. The interval you would want to call the flat phase was in fact a straight line of compound growth.

And during this run-up period, faint traces of the directions that would later stand up were already flickering, right at the bottom edge of what can be measured.

## The directions were not raised — they were chosen

So did those traces simply grow larger and become the new directions?

When I wrote the previous paper, that was my expectation: the small directions in the run-up period would be miniature versions of the completed ones.

I checked. They were not.

Comparing the directions visible during the run-up with the directions that settled after the expansion stopped, **they were almost entirely different things.**

The directions were rotating, mixing, and being reorganized in the very middle of the rapid expansion.

It resembles sculpture.

The first rough shape is not a miniature of the finished work. As the carving proceeds, the form changes again and again.

The directions were the same. They were not enlarged according to a blueprint — **they were dynamically selected inside the process of the rapid expansion.**

And almost at the same time the expansion stopped, a clear structure appeared.

In the previous article, I introduced this as new directions standing up.

This time, examining the seedless system precisely, what stood up was not just two directions.

**Including a third direction that I had not been able to see through last time, a structure of three directions settled in, together with the arrest of the expansion.**

## Will a second inflation come?

Here the next question arises.

Inflation happened once, and three directions were made.

Then should not a second inflation happen from that state, making a fourth and a fifth direction?

In fact, I had tested this in the previous paper.

Into the settled metastable state, I planted a seed once more. A second fluctuation.

The result — no second inflation.

In this paper, I went further.

I removed the second seed as well, left the system completely untouched, and extended the observation period to twice its previous length.

**Even then, not a single sign of a second inflation appeared.**

After making three directions in the first expansion, the system kept calming its oscillations, heading steadily toward a quiet, stable state.

Directions that would ring if struck from outside still remain. But nothing rings them naturally.

At least within the range of this experiment, the inflation of this system happens exactly once, and **the directions stop at three.**

## What was not born — no trace of electromagnetism

Here I want to place this against real cosmology.

According to the standard picture, the end of inflation is the beginning of the Big Bang.

And at the beginning of the Big Bang, it is said that the electromagnetic force arose, separately from gravity.

What about my system?

This system does contain a direction that can be read as a quantity conserved across the whole system, without rotating. I regard it as a candidate for a quantity corresponding to gravity.

But — and I write this honestly — unfortunately:

**No new direction beyond the three that could correspond to the electromagnetic force was born. Nor did anything like a second conserved quantity appear.**

Directions that would ring if struck remain, yet nothing stands up naturally.

Three directions, and one conserved-quantity-like direction. That was as far as this system built on its own.

## Perhaps this is not "that" inflation

From here on, this is not an experimental result. It is my conjecture.

If the inflation of this system reflects something real, then the absence of electromagnetism may not be a defect. It may be a hint.

That is, the question is this.

**Perhaps this rapid expansion shows only the expansion from the Planck-scale, minuscule domain up to the scale of atoms.**

Perhaps it is an initial inflation that comes before the inflation we usually speak of — the expansion that leads from there to the present cosmic scale.

Perhaps the birth of electromagnetism belongs to that next stage, and simply does not yet appear in this first expansion.

If this conjecture is right, a heavy realization follows.

Judging from the scale and the temporal extent of the present experimental system, there is a **scale gap of more than 10 to the 20th power** between the two.

In other words, the next stage — the stage where electromagnetism should be born — cannot be simulated at all at the size of the present experimental system.

It may be impossible even with the most powerful supercomputers of today.

Does the stop at three directions show the limit of this system? Or is it correctly cutting out exactly one stage in the history of the universe?

I cannot yet say which.

## Then what was the first fluctuation?

Strictly speaking, even the seedless system retains an infinitesimal fluctuation.

It is the rounding remainder that every computer calculation necessarily carries. The system started its growth from a remainder at the thirty-third decimal place — many orders of magnitude smaller than the seed.

So we can say this.

**The inflation of this system does not care about the size of the trigger. From however small a fluctuation, it rises at the same time, in the same shape.**

The trigger does not create the phenomenon. The system itself is the amplifier.

However, whether the same thing happens in a strictly mathematical world where even the remainder is exactly zero — that has not been proven. It is the next problem to investigate.

## What I can claim to have found, and what I cannot

What I can claim to have found this time is the following.

- Even with the seed (the assumed fluctuation) completely removed, the inflation-like rapid expansion happens
- The seed selected neither the time of the expansion nor the directions that were born
- The run-up period was not a standstill but compound growth without a single pause, step after step
- The directions were not the run-up traces simply grown large; they were reorganized and selected during the expansion
- Together with the arrest of the expansion, a three-direction structure settled in, including the third direction
- With a second seed planted, and with the seed removed and the period doubled, no second inflation occurred
- Beyond the three directions and the conserved-quantity-like direction, no new direction that could correspond to electromagnetism appeared, and no second conserved quantity

On the other hand, there are things I cannot yet claim.

- That this explains the actual inflation of the real universe
- That the three directions correspond to the length, width, and height of real space
- Which stage of the history of the universe this expansion corresponds to (the reading as an initial stage from the Planck scale to the atomic scale is my conjecture)
- That the expansion starts even in a world where the remainder is exactly zero
- Why it stops at three

The correspondence to the real universe remains at the level of a closely similar structure. Still, a system in which rapid expansion begins without any assumed fluctuation and the directions stop at three could be built from nothing but the relations among waves and the condition of closure — I believe this fact is worth putting on record.

## In one line

Last time, I showed that

**the splitting of the wave stops by finitely occupying new directions.**

This time, I removed every human hand from the whole process and showed that

**without assuming any fluctuation, the inflation-like expansion and the birth of three directions happen on their own — and happen exactly once.**

The remaining riddles have narrowed to three.

Why does the rapid expansion begin after the run-up?

Why does the reorganization choose three directions?

Why does a second one never come?

And beyond them, a still larger question is waiting.

At which stage of the universe is electromagnetism born? Is that a place today's computers can reach?

That is where I am heading next.
