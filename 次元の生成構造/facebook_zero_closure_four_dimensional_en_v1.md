Zero Closure Was Four-Dimensional — central projection survived the move to complex numbers, and the road to computing a universe without solving the many-body problem is now open (new paper published)

Details in the comments:

My series of papers has always started from a single equation: x²+y²+z²=R². Treated as real, it is nothing more than the equation of an arbitrary point on a sphere of radius R. But this form carries an enormous advantage — as long as you move on the sphere, you can compute the motion without solving the many-body problem. There is only one condition to keep: "do not change R". There is no need to chase hundreds or thousands of interactions one by one. This is what central projection means, and it is why I started here.

From there the series extended xn to complex numbers and took Σxn²=0 as the basic equation, on the principle of attaching an imaginary symbol to whatever cannot be observed and moving it to the left-hand side. A surprising amount came out of this form: that a wave of a single frequency, after a run-up period, undergoes geometric expansion and moves autonomously into a metastable state; that three directions appear in the system during that transition; that the number of waves is not fixed inside the system but set by the resolution given from outside; that an interaction with a reflection coefficient reproduces both fermionic elastic reflection and the contractive reaction of a wave packet; that near reflectance 0.7 there is a special solution whose exact root of finite-order recurrence sits close to α⁻¹≈137. And finally I reached a periodic table of 62 particle-like waves, built from the waveform alone.

But the dynamics hit a wall. Σxn²=0 contains the many-body problem for three or more bodies, so the dynamics could not be derived. It had been so easy in the real case; the moment I went complex, it became unclear whether the greatest advantage of central projection was still alive. That single point is what this paper answers: can the complex extension be solved the same way as central projection?

The answer is yes — and in a clean form. Writing xn=qn+ipn and separating real and imaginary parts, algebra alone gives Σqn²=Σpn² and Σqn·pn=0. The first is the essential one. The sum of squares of the real parts equals the sum of squares of the imaginary parts — exactly the same type of equality constraint as the real central projection Σxn²=R². Going complex did not destroy the map onto a surface. Separating what can be observed from what cannot gives x²+y²+z²=t²+R²+Q², and transposing gives r²−t²−R²−Q²=0. A light cone. That is, the basic representation is the four-dimensional (r,t,R,Q), and zero closure gives a light-cone-type null cone on it. That is the meaning of the title. And exactly as in the real case, so long as you satisfy the single constraint of conserving the right-hand side, you may move freely on the surface. The advantage of central projection was not lost in the complex world.

Something I had not expected also became clear along the way. Σxn²=0 is fundamentally an n-dimensional affair, yet the state can be represented as an arbitrary point on a three-dimensional ellipsoid. From the geometric side too: classify the 120 segments of a four-dimensional parallelotope (a skewed hypercube) into four classes by "the dimension of the smallest face containing both endpoints", attach signs, and the alternating sum lands exactly on zero, 32−96+96−32=0, with the 16 vertices lying exactly on a four-dimensional ellipsoid. The error is 7×10⁻¹⁵ — machine precision itself. A by-product: imaginary numbers turned out not to be necessary for the existence of solutions. For parallelotopes, "sum of squares of edges = sum of squares of main diagonals" holds exactly with every length real. The imaginary unit is only a notation for writing signs.

Here is what I most want to say this time. A system at resolution N=16 has 15 principal axes, and only the top three are what we see as three dimensions. So where did the other twelve go? In higher-dimensional theories the usual account is that the extra dimensions curled up small and became invisible — compactification. But that is not what happens in this system. At τ≈9000 the top three jump upward (the A axis by 2.80), the middle five barely change, and the lower ones pass through zero, drop below and become imaginary. And the decisive point: the four axes that fell to imaginary did not shrink and vanish — they changed sign while increasing in absolute value (p axis ×5.57, o axis ×2.62, n axis ×1.67). They are not shrinking. If anything, they are growing. What is happening is not "the extra dimensions curled up and disappeared" but "only three directions expanded geometrically". The top-3 occupancy goes from 0.384 to 0.812. At the very least, one can state flatly that this is not compactification of the shrinking type.

What is interesting is that even in the midst of this violent expansion, the signed trace is perfectly conserved: over the last 968 points, minimum and maximum agree to eight digits. But a misreading is forbidden here — "the conservation law holds, therefore no expansion is occurring" is wrong. The sum of the positive eigenvalues alone is not horizontal; it jumps by 1.90, and the absolute sum by 2.79. The total in the real directions grows, the total in the imaginary directions appears from zero and grows, and it is their difference that is conserved. And imaginary directions never appear on the vacuum side: in the vacuum all 15 axes are real and perfectly isotropic. Imaginary directions appear together with the creation of matter — that is the conclusion of the control experiment.

Having made strong claims, I state the boundaries too. I do not claim that the principal axes correspond to axes of real time or space (there is at present no ground whatsoever for such an identification). I do not claim that the 15 principal axes are the same thing as the 11 dimensions of superstring theory — this number is N−1, arising from the resolution, and the fact that an earlier version had 11 of them, matching the 11 of superstrings, was an accident of the choice of resolution. Nor do I claim to have derived the law of dynamics itself; what I derived is the structure of the reduction, namely which single quantity must be conserved for the motion to close.
