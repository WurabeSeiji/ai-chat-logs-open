[Why were elementary particles born? — A particle is a rare wave that completed a closure cycle in the sea of light]
See the first comment for the full article.

The Standard Model of physics explains how elementary particles behave with extraordinary precision. But it does not explain why the photon, the electron, or the quarks were born in the first place. Just like the birth of time and space, the kinds of particles are an input to the theory, not an answer of it.

This series has been an attempt to reduce that input. No background spacetime is placed at the start; the axiom is only that multiple waves satisfy the square-closure relation Σxₙ² = 0. From that alone, earlier papers derived the mechanism by which three spatial-looking directions arise spontaneously.

The new paper derives the next step: from wave shape alone, a mechanism by which there arise waves that interact bosonically, like the photon, and waves that carry mass and charge and interact fermionically, like the electron.

The central idea inverts default and exception. The default state is a sea of light — the closure axiom itself describes fully coherent, light-like material with neither mass nor charge. Light is not something to be created; the material of the system is light from the beginning. What needed explaining was matter.

Two branchings give birth to particles. First, the parity of the harmonics: waves of odd harmonics flip sign under a half-period shift — the origin of fermionic behavior. Second, the completion of a closure cycle: within the sea of light, only a chain of exchanges that happens to complete an integer-ratio period exactly becomes a recurring closed wave — a particle.

In short: a particle is a rare event — a wave that completed a closure cycle in the sea of light. Why matter amounts to only about 6 parts in 10 billion of the photons, yet is not zero, also follows automatically from counting integer-ratio pairs (a fraction of roughly 2 ln N / N). No fine-tuning is needed.

What has not been solved is stated just as plainly: why charges like 1/3 — the quark charges — arise; why the elementary charge takes its particular value (mathematically there are 60 candidate addresses); why bosons other than the photon (W, Z) exist; and the mass hierarchy — not a single mass value can yet be stated. Being unambiguous about the open problems is what makes the paper strongest.
