The Coin and Cup Thought Experiment ― Writing "a cup hides a coin" with nothing but overlapping waves

Details in the comments

I asked whether the cup and coin of Professor Masahiro Hotta's note article "The Linear Algebra of Quantum Mechanics Is Merely a Notation for Probability" could be reproduced with the interference of waves alone, without assuming a background spacetime, and published the result as a paper that contains hypotheses
It does not object to the conclusion of the article; what I borrowed is only the setting of a cup and a coin
At first, harmonics were added on a circle S¹, with a small peak as the coin and a large peak as the cup. The cross term (the product of the two waves) stands only when they overlap
But although overlapping could be told apart from not overlapping, the coin being visible could not be told apart from the coin being hidden
What came to me after a night's sleep was to make a wave not of the cup but of the places where the cup is not. As a wave shape, this is a well
Then the cross term alone gave: hidden when the cup is placed over the coin, partly visible when the rim lies over it, visible when shifted away. Neither a three-dimensional spherical shell S³ nor an observer had to be added
At this point the question was replaced, from whether the coin is there or not to whether it is visible or not. I gave up distinguishing "not visible" from "not there"
What we learn when the cup is placed over the coin is only that the coin is no longer visible; whether there is a coin inside the cup, we do not know
For the cup to be movable while remaining the same cup, the system must be finite, closed, and without ends
The wave shapes and the numbers were checked with the attached programs. Reading an act as a wave, and the narrowing down of the shape of the system, are hypotheses; their verification is still to come
