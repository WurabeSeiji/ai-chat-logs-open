# Why Were Elementary Particles Born? — A Particle Is a Rare Wave That Completed a Closure Cycle in the Sea of Light

Noriaki Kihara
August 4, 2026

The Standard Model of physics explains an astonishing amount. How elementary particles behave, how they collide, with what probabilities they decay — the precision is extraordinary.

But there is something the Standard Model does not explain.

**Why were the elementary particles — the photon, the electron, the quarks — born in the first place?**

This question sits outside the theory, just like the question of why time and space were born. The kinds of particles are not an answer of the theory; they are an input to it.

This series has been an attempt to reduce that input.

We place no background spacetime at the start. The axiom is only this: among multiple waves there holds the square-closure relation Σxₙ² = 0. From that alone, earlier papers derived the mechanism by which three directions that look spatial arise spontaneously (the inflation-like rapid expansion started even with every seed removed, and the directions stopped at three).

The new paper derives the next step.

**From wave shape alone, a mechanism by which there arise waves that interact bosonically, like the photon, and waves that carry mass and charge and interact fermionically, like the electron.**

## The central idea: invert default and exception

Ordinarily one thinks: first there are particles, and particles emit light.

In this framework it is the reverse.

**The default state is a sea of light.** The square-closure axiom itself describes fully coherent shared waves carrying neither mass nor charge — light-like material. Light is not something to be created; the material of the system is light from the very beginning.

What needed explaining was matter.

## The two branchings that give birth to particles

**The first branching is the parity of the harmonics.** A wave is built out of many harmonics (just like the timbre of a musical instrument). A wave made of odd-numbered harmonics flips sign when shifted by half a period, and this is the origin of fermionic behavior. Waves of even-numbered harmonics are single-valued — bosonic. No statistics need to be put in from outside.

**The second branching is the completion of a closure cycle.** Within the sea of light, when a chain of exchanges between waves happens to complete an integer-ratio period exactly, that wave becomes a closed wave that recurs exactly to its original state — a particle. Relations that fail to complete remain in the sea.

In other words:

**A particle is a rare event — a wave that completed a closure cycle in the sea of light.**

## Photon and electron, written out by just four properties

In this framework, the kind of a particle is decided by four properties of its wave shape: the parity of its harmonics, integer-ratio binding, coherence (whether it is fully shared), and hair (the presence of the ± carriers that hold the sign).

The photon is a hair-free, fully shared even-difference wave. Fully shared, so its mass is zero (because mass is the breaking of sharing — incoherence). Hair-free, so its charge is zero. It holds no periodic address, so it is unbound and flies free.

An electron-type wave is made of odd harmonics (fermionic), bound to an integer-ratio address (the angle of that address is the value of its charge — the computed value 0.302822 agrees with the dimensionless elementary charge of physics to 16 parts in 100 million), its full sharing is broken (it has mass), and it carries hair (it has plus and minus).

And matter and antimatter are given in pairs automatically by the geometry of the completed cycle: at the halfway point of the cycle the sign flips, and at the full cycle it returns to itself — there is no need to prepare particles and antiparticles separately.

## Why matter is almost absent, yet not zero

For every photon in the universe, there are only about 6 parts in 10 billion of matter (baryons). Why so few — and yet not zero?

In this framework, counting alone gives the shape of the answer. A system of resolution N has about N²/2 relational waves, but only integer-ratio pairs can complete a closure cycle, and their fraction is roughly 2 ln N / N. As N grows, it falls steeply — but it never reaches zero. The rarity of matter is not the product of fine-tuning; it is the rarity of integer ratios itself.

Setting the observed ratio of 6 parts in 10 billion equal to this fraction reads off the resolution at the moment the expansion stopped: N ≈ 10 to the 11th. (This is a reading from observation, not a derivation — let me state that honestly.)

## What has not been solved — stated plainly

The paper lists what it has not achieved with the same clarity as what it has.

**Why charges like 1/3 — the quark charges — arise is not understood.** The charge this framework derives is the value of an integer-ratio address; the fractional charges of quarks, and the structure of the strong force that confines them (the SU(3) type), are still out of reach.

**Why the elementary charge takes its particular value is not understood either.** Mathematically there are 60 candidate addresses, and why only one of them — the one giving 0.302822 — is realized is the largest open problem of this series.

**Why more complex particles are born is not understood.** The reason bosons other than the photon exist (the massive W and Z, for instance) has not been derived; it is marked explicitly as a vacant slot in the decision tree.

**The mass hierarchy and the energy hierarchy are not understood.** We can say that mass arises as the breaking of sharing, but why the generations — electron, muon, tau — line up discretely, and why the masses have the values they do: **not a single mass value can yet be stated.**

These are recorded at the end of the paper as ten open problems in three tiers. Being unambiguous about the open problems is, I believe, what makes this paper strongest.

## In one line

Previously, this series showed inflation without assumed fluctuations and the birth of three directions, and then the spontaneous separation of gravity and the electric force.

On top of that, this paper shows:

**The kinds of particles are not an input. In the sea of light, from wave shape alone — the parity of harmonics and the completion of integer-ratio cycles — bosonic waves and fermionic waves separate spontaneously.**

The big remaining mysteries: the selection of the address (why the elementary charge has its value), the fractional charges of quarks, the values of the masses, and the bosons other than the photon. That is where we go next.
