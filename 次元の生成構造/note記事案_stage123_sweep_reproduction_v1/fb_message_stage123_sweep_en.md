# The Dynamics That Drives Inflation Is Made of Three Parts

See comments for details

In July I reported a phenomenon in which an unmeasurably small seed (10⁻³⁰) amplifies itself exponentially over dozens of orders of magnitude. I have now decomposed that dynamics into three parts.
Part 1 is the device that rotates the collection of waves, with its clock. Part 2 is a normalization that discards each wave's magnitude and builds the interaction from directions alone. Part 3 is an operation that extracts only half of the interaction, making the rotation a real rotation.
Amplification occurs only when Part 2 and Part 3 are both present.
Remove either one, and noise far larger than the seed is injected at the very first step; the amplification can no longer be observed.
The shortcut of normalizing just once at the start breaks most violently of all. Normalization is a rule applied at every step.
Running 228 cases across system sizes N=3 to 40, the first step stayed at the seed scale in every run, and most of them showed the same amplification curve as in July. This is not a coincidence of one particular system.
In the final state, the directions of the waves scatter in all directions while their magnitudes line up almost perfectly.
The paper contains no physical interpretation. A three-stage bit-for-bit cross-check against the July computation and a SHA256 ledger of every file fix a record that anyone can use to reach the same result.
