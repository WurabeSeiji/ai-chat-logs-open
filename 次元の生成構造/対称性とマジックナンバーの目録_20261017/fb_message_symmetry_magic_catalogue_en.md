Do symmetries carry dimensionless numbers rather than conserved quantities? A catalogue of symmetries and magic numbers for reading out spacetime from a state space

Details in the comments

My research reads out spacetime and particles from a set of complex numbers alone, with no background spacetime. I could not draw the map and was wandering, so I reorganized everything from the axioms and the symmetries
The square z′ = z² of a complex number z is just a complex number too. A readout cannot tell which layer a value belongs to
So the zero closure of the sum of squares, Σz² = 0, is exactly the zero closure of the linear sum of z′, Σz′ = 0. The two closures were one definition
Waves travel without a tail only in odd dimensions, so space can close while staying three-dimensional and continuous. For the phase to return, spacetime becomes four-dimensional and discrete
The same conserved quantity is called charge, energy, mass or speed depending on the side. In a system without name tags, separate symmetries become different views of one symmetry
In this system every quantity is fixed only as a ratio (an angle), so it is scale invariant. However the ruler is exchanged, what survives is only the dimensionless numbers fixed by the structure
So I propose the hypothesis that symmetries carry not conserved quantities but dimensionless magic numbers. I also list nine places where Noether's theorem fails or is doubtful
When different symmetries call for nearby numbers, the observed value settles on none of them: close, but not matching. The three 137s have this form
The map from the state space to spacetime is hard because what can be read is not decided until the projection is chosen. Since spacetime reads stably, the map is at least a partial projection
I mapped all 156 published papers to the entries, renumbered the axioms, and revised an earlier claim that α matches exactly into "close but not matching"
