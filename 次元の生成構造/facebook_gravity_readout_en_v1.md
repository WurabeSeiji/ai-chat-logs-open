Where Was Gravity Mislaid? — A Century of Struggle to Unify Quantum Theory and Gravity May Have Been a Mistake of Placement (New Paper Released)

Details in the comments:

Physics carries a wound that has not closed for a hundred years: the unification of quantum mechanics and gravity. Try to make the two theories one, and the calculations blow up to infinity; renormalization, which works for the other forces, simply refuses to work for gravity. This paper proposes a diagnosis for that wound — the cause of the struggle may lie not in the content of the theories, but in where gravity was placed.

It begins with my own failure. My previous paper, "The Periodic Table of Waves," had no dynamics. So this time, without building anything in by hand, I put a gravitational potential directly into the universal interaction function of the waves themselves — and it failed, clearly. This wave system runs while preserving an exact zero closure, Σxn²=0 (the bare dynamics keeps it to 10 to the minus 13). Inject a potential into the state, and the closure breaks by 31%. And the failure is mathematically inevitable: one can prove that only operations with antisymmetric generators preserve the closure for every state (the tangent theorem). The intriguing part is that the injection preserves the size of the state perfectly while breaking the closure — which is exactly why the breakage is so hard to see.

So I turned the idea inside out. In this series, space, time, position — all of them arose as quantities read out from the wave state. Then acceleration too should be a phenomenon of the readout side. Separate waves and fields into two layers: the wave layer (existence, preserving zero closure exactly) and the field layer (one readout function reading space, time, mass, and charge — zero tuning parameters). Put gravitational distortion into the readout layer: warping the readout gauges never touches the state, so the closure stays intact. The result: the unification of gauge fields close in concept to the standard theory with gravitationally distorted fields nearly succeeded, via the universal field-readout function.

Out of that one function, everything followed, each with measured numbers: the vacuum does not tick; the law of mass became a theorem (a prediction that runs no dynamics matched 15 measured points at ratio 0.983 with 1.2% scatter — the structure of the Higgs mechanism as a measured fact); what falling really is (the gradient of the clock accelerates the momentum of the sea); attraction in all pairs, 30 wins and 0 losses; the graviton's bilinear signature (the clock field picks up only the fields' difference frequencies, 0.0% error); and the decisive experiment — a charge-blind gravity channel and a charge-structure-dependent gauge-like channel emerging from the same readout, separately and at once. The separation is proven as a theorem, and the selection rule for which pairs open a channel coincided exactly with the winding-walk law of the periodic table. A number-theoretic prediction was then tested in a long-time experiment: the forbidden leakage came out at 8.9×10 to the minus 26 — zero at machine precision.

Looking back, the diagnosis reads: the struggle to unify quantum theory and gravity came from bringing acceleration into the dynamics of particles, generating divergences, and erasing them with renormalization, over and over. A counterterm is the price of confusing the layers. And the standard theory's practice of stacking gauge theory upon gauge theory rested on the same one-layer ontology. One more byproduct: mass is the value of the clock field, gravity its gradient — mass and gravity are the 0th and 1st derivatives of one and the same clock field.

This is, of course, a framework proposal. Proofs, measurements, empirical rules, and hypotheses are labeled as such in every section; all 13 refutations are kept in the paper (including three records of my own newer theorems refuting my own earlier claims); the remaining problems are registered as a 10-item verification program. The paper is published on Zenodo in both Japanese and English (CC BY 4.0), together with every experimental program and the figure-generation code, so anyone can reproduce and refute it.
