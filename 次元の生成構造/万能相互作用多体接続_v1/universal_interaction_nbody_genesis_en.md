## 5. Conditional Derivations

### 5.1 The mediated-vertex uniqueness theorem

For the most general form in the assumed class (two-wave, pointwise, cubic, common-phase invariant, no self-scattering), $\delta z_e = i\sum_{e'\sim e}(g_1 R_{ee'}|z_{e'}|^2 z_e + g_2 R'_{ee'} z_{e'}^2\bar z_e)$, conservation of the zero-square-sum closure $\sum_e z_e^2$ (per register point) forces:

$$g_2 = -g_1 \quad\text{and}\quad R_{ee'} = R_{e'e}\ (\text{pair symmetry}).$$

The proof is by pairwise cancellation of the first variation: the contribution of the edge pair $(e,e')$ is $g_1 R_{ee'}|z_{e'}|^2 z_e^2 + g_2 R'_{ee'} z_{e'}^2 |z_e|^2$ plus its $e\leftrightarrow e'$ exchange, and it vanishes identically only when the coefficients are opposite and the strengths pair-symmetric. Three points. First, this is uniqueness within the class. Second, the two-body $g_2=-g_1$ [1] is the $M=2$ special case (the adjacency sum has one partner). Third, closure demands of the strengths **pair symmetry only**: as the mixing rule when two edges have different $R$, both the arithmetic mean and the product conserve closure exactly (machine test T5). That the actual dynamics is consistent with the arithmetic mean is an additional experimental fact.

**Extension theorem T-L**: since pair symmetry holds per point $n$, register-point-dependent pair-symmetric strengths $R_{ee'}(n)$ also conserve closure exactly. Machine check: drift $1.0\times10^{-17}$ (machine zero) for random $n$-dependent pair-symmetric $R$; $1.7\times10^{-2}$ (broken) when asymmetric. **Locally varying interaction strength is compatible with the axiom** — the axiom does not forbid locality.

### 5.2 The identity of the computational structure — anonymity guarantees computability

Naively, evaluating the adjacency sum scans $2(N-2)$ adjacent edges per edge, costing $O(M\cdot N)=O(N^3)$ per register point. But by (ii) two-wave additivity (no cross terms between partners) and the separability of the pair-symmetric mean strength, the adjacency sum folds **exactly**: for edge $e=(u,v)$,

$$\sum_{e'\sim e} |z_{e'}|^2 = A_u + A_v - 2|z_e|^2, \qquad A_v \equiv \sum_{e\ni v}|z_e|^2$$

(likewise for the four ledgers including the $z^2$-type and $R$-weighted ones). The computation is two passes — "edges → node aggregation" and "nodes → edge read-back with self-subtraction" — each $O(M)$. This is an identity, not a mean-field approximation; agreement with the naive implementation is verified to machine precision. The total per step is $O(M\cdot N_{reg}\log N_{reg})$ — the same order as the trivial lower bound in the RAM model of having to read the whole state once per step, and in this sense the theoretical lower bound.

The physical meaning: information flows edge → shared node → edge. **The interaction is not a pairwise direct check but aggregation and redistribution mediated by the bodies** — the $O(M)$ algorithm is the literal implementation of "mediated vertex." And what makes the folding possible is anonymity itself: give each pair its own coupling table $R_{ee'}$ (violating anonymity) and separability dies, returning the cost to $O(N^3)$. A physical principle directly guarantees a computational property.

**Measured conserved quantities** (under the discipline of deriving the invariant before judging): relative norm drift $3.1\times10^{-14}$; vertex-only pointwise closure drift $7.3\times10^{-11}$; linear-only slice closure drift $1.4\times10^{-15}$ (each judged only where it is an invariant; see Section 12). The ON/OFF separation of generated content is 11 orders; the ON amount is consistent with the value derived from the ignition law within a factor 4.2.

## 13. Prior Work — Correspondences Found After Independent Construction

The architecture of this paper was constructed independently from the two axioms and the two-body vertex, not designed by relying on the following. Re-surveying after the design, we found these correspondences. They are supporting evidence that the framework is not an isolated fantasy; they are not proofs of correctness.

1. **Variables on edges — lattice gauge theory** [8]. In Wilson's lattice gauge theory the gauge field (link variables) lives on edges, with interactions built through sites and plaquettes. Differences: a background lattice (fixed geometry) and a gauge group are inputs there, and matter fields live on sites; here there is no geometry (complete graph), groups are post-hoc classifications, and nodes carry no variables at all.
2. **Spacetime emergent from pairwise degrees of freedom — matrix models (IKKT/BFSS)** [9]. The fundamental degrees of freedom are matrices whose off-diagonal entries are strings connecting brane pairs (pairwise relational degrees of freedom); there is no ambient spacetime, and in Lorentzian IKKT Monte Carlo the nine-dimensional symmetry breaks spontaneously to an expanding three-dimensional space [10] — phenomenologically parallel to the three-direction emergence of this series. Differences: the action comes from superstring theory (supersymmetry as input); matrices carry diagonals (per-body position variables) whereas we are purely relational; and the address of locality differs (next item).
3. **Complete graphs and locality — quantum graphity** [11]. Identical to us up to the starting point — degrees of freedom on a complete graph with physics invariant under vertex permutations (= anonymity) — graphity proposed that locality emerges by the graph thinning out in a low-energy phase. Our measurement is **the opposite answer to the same question**: the graph stays complete, locality does not appear on it (Section 9), and it lives on the register axis.
4. **Edge oscillators with node mediation — topological Kuramoto** [12]. The recent framework placing phase oscillators on edges coupled through Hodge Laplacians (node sharing) is the closest mathematical form to our adjacency structure. Differences: their coupling is gradient-type and the subject is synchronization; ours is the cubic exchange flow selected by closure conservation, and the subject is generation.
5. **Exact reduction of all-to-all coupling — Kuramoto mean field** [13]. The order-parameter reduction $O(N^2)\to O(N)$ is the classical precedent of our node ledgers.
