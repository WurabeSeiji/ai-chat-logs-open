Read two complex numbers as one quaternion, and spacetime (1+3) appears. A map from a set of complex numbers to spacetime

Details in the comments

My research reads out spacetime from a set of complex numbers {zₙ} alone, with no background spacetime. I have now built the map {zₙ} (2N) → quaternions {qₙ} (N) → spacetime (1+3)
Two complex numbers make a quaternion q = z₁ + z₂ j. A quaternion is just a four-dimensional vector, but read through multiplication it splits into 1+3
The polar form of a quaternion, q = |q|(cos θ + n sin θ), is exactly the central projection onto the tangent plane at the north pole, u = v/q₀ = n tan θ
The real part of the unconjugated true square, q₀² − |v|², has signature (1, 3), and θ = 45° is the light cone. No metric is placed from outside
A configuration that treats the four components equally does not close under the true square. The 16-cell {±1, ±i, ±j, ±k} is isotropic, but the sum of its true squares is −4
The most symmetric configuration is q = ±e^(π n/4) (n the vertex directions of a regular polyhedron): each lies on the light cone, with q² = n and q⁸ = 1. The 24 quaternions of the icosahedron are the most symmetric
The directions that N quaternions can open as space number min(3, N−2), stopping at three. The smallest number that opens three directions isotropically is N = 5
This map is a partial projection. The size, the ± sign, the equator and the global rotation escape the readout
Names (time t, space xyz) are not colours attached to the quaternion from the start; they are attached afterwards to properties of the readout
