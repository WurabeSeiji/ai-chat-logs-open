# Wave separation test under finite closure U^K = I
## Purpose
Test whether prime/coprime harmonic indices are sufficient to define two separated waves in a finite cyclic closure.
## Exact quantities
- `order_a = K/gcd(K,m_a)`
- `order_b = K/gcd(K,m_b)`
- `pair_order = lcm(order_a,order_b) = K/gcd(K,m_a,m_b)`
- `crt_independent = (pair_order = order_a*order_b)`, equivalently `gcd(order_a,order_b)=1`.
- The smallest cyclic subgroup containing both modes is generated by `U^d` with `d=gcd(K,m_a,m_b)`. Thus every pair remains inside one cyclic subgroup; prime indices alone do not create two algebraically unrelated generators.
## Key result
Prime or coprime exponents are **not sufficient** for discrete independence. The stronger exact criterion for the synchronous pair orbit to cover every combination of the two individual phase cycles is `gcd(order_a,order_b)=1`. This is the Chinese-remainder (CRT) factorization condition.

## Interpretation for the second paper
A useful hierarchy is:
1. Same finite closure: all modes lie in a finite cyclic group `Z_K`.
2. Same cyclic subgroup: determined by `d=gcd(K,m_a,m_b)`; this always provides a common generator `U^d`.
3. Discrete phase independence: the pair orbit realizes the full product of the individual cycles iff their orders are coprime.
4. Therefore a prime relation such as 2:3 is only a candidate; whether it behaves as two separable phase degrees depends on K. Example: K=6 gives orders 3 and 2, hence full CRT independence; K=12 gives orders 6 and 4, hence no independence.
