# Do Elementary Particles Carry a ROM, RAM, and CPU? — Connecting Quantum Mechanics, Gravity, and the Four Forces from a Single Interaction-Free Rule (Part I Full Paper & Part II Unified Connection Hypothesis)

(Japanese version / 日本語版: https://note.com/kiharanoriaki/n/n09cbc13c6be5)

For many years, one simple question about physics never quite left my mind:

"How does an elementary particle know what kind of particle it is, and how does it respond only to the exact forces meant for it?"

An electron responds to electromagnetism, yet completely ignores the strong nuclear force (QCD). A neutrino ignores both electromagnetism and the strong force. Quarks respond to both. On top of that, every particle carries its position-like information, momentum, and internal clock (phase) across space with remarkable fidelity, rarely losing them.

It almost looks as if every single elementary particle has built-in hardware:
- A **ROM** storing its species and the laws of physics it must obey
- A **RAM** preserving its position, momentum, and internal clock without corruption
- A **CPU** computing how to interact whenever it meets another particle

The Standard Model and General Relativity write down the specification sheet with astonishing precision. Yet neither tells us where that ROM, RAM, or CPU is actually implemented—or whether Nature really engineered such an intricate machine inside every particle.

Inspired in part by the AI-proposed gravity hypothesis discussed on November 25, I tackled this long-standing question directly. The result is now published on Zenodo in two companion papers: **Part I** (a unified full paper synthesizing seven rapid communications) and **Part II** (a unified connection hypothesis bridging gravity, electromagnetism, QCD, and Dirac matter).

## A Shift in Perspective: Don't Separate "State" from "Interaction"

In virtually all conventional physics—no matter how advanced—we put two separate ingredients in by hand: the **state** (particles, wavefunctions, fields) and the **interaction law** (Hamiltonians, actions, coupling constants).

This time, we stripped all of that away:

- No background spacetime (neither 3D space nor time)
- No complex numbers and no quantum probability rule (Born rule)
- No assumed interactions, coupling constants, or fermion fields

All we kept was a real-valued 3D cube of numbers (a rank-3 tensor T) and a single one-line self-consistency rule:

"Contract T with itself three times around a triangle loop, and require it to close self-consistently (the triangle-loop product Tri(T))."

Here is the crucial twist: a rank-3 tensor T is simultaneously the **state** AND the **multiplication table (interaction rule)** itself. Without writing any force law from the outside, **the state spontaneously generates its own self-consistent interactions**.

How do we connect gravity, electromagnetism, the strong force (QCD), and Dirac matter into one picture from there?

Now return to the original mystery:

How can a particle know its species, respond only to the right forces, and preserve its information without carrying a ROM, RAM, or CPU?

The answer is astonishingly economical:

- **No ROM inside the particle**: A particle does not "remember" a rulebook. Its species is simply which algebraic seat (sector) it occupies in the octonion structure, and that geometric seat automatically determines which forces it resonates with—just as an overtone on a string doesn't need to memorize its frequency to know which chords it harmonizes with.
- **RAM is protected by symmetry**: Algebraic conservation laws—L² invariance, phase rigidity, and Bianchi identities—act as built-in memory protection keeping information intact.
- **No external CPU**: There is no separate engine executing laws on particles; there is only the single one-line self-consistency loop Tri(T) where state and interaction are the same object.

Rather than engineering miniature computers inside every particle, Nature may have chosen the ultimate energy-saving design: one self-consistency rule that spontaneously generates the algebraic structure, from which everything else unfolds.
