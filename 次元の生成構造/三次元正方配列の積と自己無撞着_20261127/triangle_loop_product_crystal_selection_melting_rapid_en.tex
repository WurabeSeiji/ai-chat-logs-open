higher-dimensional three-index cubic arrays, together with all
reproducible source code, datasets, and plotting scripts.

\(N \times N \times N\) cubic array \(T\):
\[\mathrm{Tri}(T)_{ijk} = \sum_{p,q,r=0}^{N-1} T_{ipq}\, T_{qjr}\, T_{rpk}.\]

\subsection{1. Motivation}\label{motivation}

The author has been using AI to investigate whether spacetime,
particles, and interactions (observation) can be derived solely from
relational structures of states---without assuming any background
spacetime---in a manner consistent with the Standard Model, General
Relativity, and other established theories {[}K1--K5{]}.

Until recently, relatively few studies pursued a strictly
background-free starting point. However, on 2026-11-25, V.
Chandrasekaran released the preprint \emph{``An algebraic quantization
of gravity from the null cone''} (arXiv:2611.34924v1) {[}C1{]},
prominently featuring AI-assisted theoretical work. A full-text
comparison revealed substantial points of contact with the author's
research: a formulation without a fixed background metric,
finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces for finite regions, corner edge-mode
frames with triple gluing consistency conditions, and subspace
separation via null states (exact citation points and contrasts are
detailed in §9).

Accordingly, the author decided to release this rapid communication
reporting interim results from extending the previously published
complex-number numerical inflation experiments on Zenodo {[}K1, K2{]} to
octonions and three-dimensional cubic arrays. A major strength of this
report is that \textbf{every single experimental result is accompanied
by deterministic, randomness-free computation scripts, raw numerical
data (CSV/NPZ/TXT), dedicated plotting scripts, and rendered figures},
enabling straightforward independent verification by any third party.

As a note on methodology, this investigation initially began with
another AI (Claude Code), which stalled in dead ends involving 2D matrix
multiplication and a misinterpretation of ``exponentiation.'' After
handing the redesign of the algebraic operation over to Google Gemini 4
Neon, the entire sequence---from conceiving the triangle-loop product to
proving analytical theorems, completing the exhaustive 1,024-case
classification, 20,000-step long-term tracking, and the \(N = 64\)
large-crystal melting and view-angle spectral measurements---was
completed in roughly half a day. For full transparency, the record of
the earlier AI's failed attempts and detours is also preserved in the
repository {[}R1{]}.

\subsubsection{2.2 Why 2D Matrix Multiplication Cannot Reach This Regime
{[}Known /
Verified{]}}\label{why-2d-matrix-multiplication-cannot-reach-this-regime-known-verified}

Left multiplication \(L(x)\) by a single octonion \(x\) can be written
as an \(8 \times 8\) real matrix, but ordinary matrix multiplication is
associative, so \(L(x)L(y) = L(xy)\) fails for distinct octonions
\textbf{{[}Known{]}}. Moreover, squaring a single matrix
\(M \mapsto M^2\) permanently fixes the eigenspaces determined by the
initial matrix \(M\), making it impossible in principle for a small
defect in a highly symmetric crystal to trigger a phase transition and
spontaneous arrest into a different metastable remnant.

\subsection{3. Definition and Basic Properties of the Triangle-Loop
Product}\label{definition-and-basic-properties-of-the-triangle-loop-product}

We replace the star contraction with the \textbf{triangle-loop product},
in which three internal indices \((p, q, r)\) are shared pairwise among
the three factors to form a closed triangular loop:

\[\mathrm{Tri}(T)_{ijk} = \sum_{p,q,r=0}^{N-1} T_{ipq}\, T_{qjr}\, T_{rpk}. \tag{1}\]

This operation satisfies the following exact properties
\textbf{{[}Analytically Proven{]}}:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Anonymity and Cyclic Symmetry:} The three external legs
  \((i, j, k)\) and three internal edges \((p, q, r)\) are completely
  symmetric, and \(\mathrm{Tri}\) is covariant under simultaneous
  permutations of element indices.
\item
  \textbf{Cubic Homogeneity:} For any scalar \(s\),
  \(\mathrm{Tri}(s T) = s^3 \mathrm{Tri}(T)\). Therefore, factoring out
  a common scalar \(s_n = \|\mathrm{Tri}(U_{n-1})\|_F\) during numerical
  tracking to prevent floating-point overflow---recording the direction
  \(U_n = \mathrm{Tri}(U_{n-1}) / s_n\) and growth factor
  \(\|P_n\| = s_n\) separately---is an exact equivalence that leaves all
  ratios and directions strictly unchanged.
\item
  \textbf{Tensor-Product Covariance:} For the Kronecker-type tensor
  product
  \((A \otimes B)_{(i_1 i_2)(j_1 j_2)(k_1 k_2)} = A_{i_1 j_1 k_1} B_{i_2 j_2 k_2}\)
  of an \(m^3\) array \(A\) and an \(n^3\) array \(B\),
  \[\mathrm{Tri}(A \otimes B) = \mathrm{Tri}(A) \otimes \mathrm{Tri}(B). \tag{2}\]
\item
  \textbf{Fast Exact Evaluation:} Equation (1) decomposes exactly into
  two BLAS matrix multiplications
  (\(X_{ipjr} = \sum_q T_{ipq} T_{qjr}\),
  \(R_{ijk} = \sum_{r,p} X_{ijrp} T_{rpk}\)) at \(O(N^5)\) cost, running
  in \(\sim 64\text{ ms}\) per step at \(N = 64\) (verified against
  direct \texttt{einsum} at \(N = 8\) to error \(< 10^{-13}\)).
\end{enumerate}
