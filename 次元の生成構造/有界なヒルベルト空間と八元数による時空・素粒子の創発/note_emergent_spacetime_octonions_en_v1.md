# Building Spacetime and Elementary Particles Without Assuming Background Spacetime — A Thought Experiment from Photon Stationarity to Central Projection, Quaternions, and Octonions

I have been pursuing a thought experiment that asks whether spacetime, elementary particles, and interactions can be reconstructed solely from state relations in a bounded, finite Hilbert space and the algebraic structures of quaternions and octonions, without positing a four-dimensional background spacetime as a pre-existing stage.

At first glance, such a starting point looks far removed from standard textbooks. Yet in both its problem consciousness and its mathematical tools, it connects to active currents in modern theoretical physics, including emergent spacetime and division-algebra approaches to particle physics.

I have now published a paper on Zenodo that brings together this line of thought experiments and dialogue—from the early central-projection model to open problems in quaternionic and octonionic dynamics (Concept DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24267347). In this article, I trace the four stages of the argument and the boundary between established mathematics and open hypotheses.

## Overview: Four Stages

The manuscript builds its working hypotheses in four stages on top of established mathematical structures:

- Stage 1: Minkowski metric (x, y, z, t, τ) and cross-sections of a 5-dimensional light cone → zero proper time of photons (ds = 0) and time progression of matter
- Stage 2: Central projection (Beltrami coordinates) via imaginary time R² = t² + (iτ)² → de Sitter spacetime, event horizon R, and gravitational distortion
- Stage 3: Quaternions ℍ (two conjugate complex numbers) and the Hopf fibration S¹ ↪ S³ → S² → retaining 3-direction phase gradients without external coordinates (photons)
- Stage 4: Octonions 𝕆 (two conjugate quaternions) and the Hopf fibration S³ ↪ S⁷ → S⁴ → closed 4-dimensional spacetime S⁴, 3 internal degrees of freedom, and self-organization of condensates

## Stage 1: Photons Stationary in Four-Dimensional Spacetime

The starting point is a simple question: why do photons always appear to travel at the speed of light?

In the Minkowski metric of special relativity,

　ds² = −(cdt)² + dx² + dy² + dz²

photons always satisfy ds = 0. From the perspective of proper time τ in four-dimensional spacetime, a photon's proper time does not advance—it is equivalent to being stationary within four-dimensional spacetime.

Rewriting this relation with proper time τ as (x² + y² + z²) + τ² = t² opens up a view of four-dimensional spacetime as a cross-section of a five-dimensional light cone.

## Stage 2: Central Projection and de Sitter Spacetime

Introducing imaginary time iτ gives

　t² − τ² = t² + (iτ)² = R²

From this relation, the space x² + y² + z² = R² emerges as a central projection (Beltrami coordinates) from a sphere of radius R, connecting directly to de Sitter spacetime and the structure of an event horizon R.

Within this framework, subjective space appears indistinguishable to an internal observer, while gravity can be interpreted as a local contraction of the curvature radius R caused by matter (a working hypothesis).

## Stage 3: Extending from Complex Numbers to Quaternions and Octonions

Next, we ask how three spatial directions can be retained without borrowing external background coordinates.

Complex numbers ℂ have only 2 degrees of freedom—insufficient to hold 3 spatial directions externally. Extending the number system via the Cayley–Dickson construction leads to quaternions ℍ (4 degrees of freedom), formed from a pair of conjugate complex numbers (z₁, z₂) ∈ ℂ². When normalized to unit length, they form the 3-sphere S³, and the Hopf fibration S¹ ↪ S³ → S² encodes the three directions of the 2-sphere S² without external coordinates. Mathematically, this is identical to the Bloch sphere representation of a single qubit (and the Poincaré sphere for photon polarization) (Appendix 1).

To handle both 4 spacetime degrees of freedom and internal degrees of freedom together, we proceed to octonions 𝕆 (8 degrees of freedom), built from a pair of conjugate quaternions. By Hurwitz's theorem, normed division algebras over the reals exist only in dimensions 1, 2, 4, and 8, making octonions the natural endpoint of this extension and giving rise to the next Hopf fibration, S³ ↪ S⁷ → S⁴.

## Stage 4: Eliminating Background Spacetime and Self-Organization

We then invert the perspective: instead of positing spacetime beforehand, start solely from state relations in a bounded, finite Hilbert space.

The existence of a curvature radius R requires space to be bounded and closed (a compact sphere S⁴). Even starting from all-to-all couplings among N states (N²), the algebraic structures of octonions—non-associativity, triality, minimal ideals, and Peirce decomposition—may factorize the system into localized condensates that survive mutual cancellation. This is the working hypothesis of Stage 4.

## Connection to Harmonics (Appendix 2)

In my earlier work, I explored how harmonics (such as sums of odd harmonics) are needed to give waves spatial localization, and how harmonic circulation relates to mass acquisition and particle lifetimes.

Octonions alone (with 8 degrees of freedom) may lack enough degrees of freedom to account for mass hierarchies and the variety of particle species. Integrating octonionic algebraic factorization (the framework of individuals and symmetries) with harmonic localization and spectral structure (the spatial profile of condensates and mass hierarchies) remains a key open problem.

## Where Mathematics Ends and Hypotheses Begin

In an independent thought experiment, drawing a clear boundary between established mathematics and working hypotheses is essential. At the end of the paper, this boundary is organized into four categories:

- Identities & Theorems: Minkowski metric (ds = 0), Cayley–Dickson construction, Hurwitz's theorem (dimensions 1, 2, 4, 8), Hopf fibrations S³ → S² and S⁷ → S⁴
- Readings & Interpretations: photon stationarity and 5D light-cone cross-sections, central projection vs. de Sitter spacetime, degree-of-freedom requirements from complex numbers to quaternions and octonions, compactness of the sphere
- Hypotheses: gravity as local contraction of curvature radius R, condensate factorization from a bounded Hilbert space via octonionic non-associativity, triality, and minimal ideals
- Open Problems: concrete update rules rewriting internal phases, mathematical proof of factorization from N² all-to-all couplings into local condensates, and unifying harmonics (localized waves and harmonic circulation) with octonionic condensates, mass hierarchies, and particle species
