The inflation was already written into the tilting wave — three independent measurements agree on one number, and one axiom fell away (new paper and a general-audience article are out)

Details in the comments

In "The inflation happened without a seed" and "A universe cannot begin from noise", I reported that my wave model starts a rapid expansion without any fluctuation seed planted by hand, and that the only thing able to trigger it is a pure wave in which everything rides on a single vibration. Three questions remained. Why does it start? What is growing? Why does it stop? The new paper answers all three with numbers.

First, what grew. The pure wave initially sits on one plane, and what grows during the expansion is the amplitude outside that plane — in the new directions. A component that starts at 10⁻³² of the total climbs to 0.1–0.4 within a few hundred steps: about 31 orders of magnitude, all at once. Yet the total does not grow by a single cent. The time evolution is a Cayley transform of a real antisymmetric matrix, which is always a real orthogonal matrix, so the total size of the wave and the closure condition (zero sum of squares) are conserved exactly, not just numerically. What grew came from the original plane. The expansion is a transfer, and the fact that it cannot exceed the total was decided by arithmetic before any dynamics. Half of "why does it stop" disappears right there.

Second, why it starts. The computation that builds the pure wave stops very slightly off the exact balance. Varying that offset deliberately over a range of a million, the smaller the offset, the later the onset — the delay is exactly proportional to the logarithm of the offset (R² = 0.99999) — while the speed of the expansion does not change at all. A pencil about to fall: the smaller the tilt, the later it starts falling, but it falls the same way.

Then one step further. Just as the fall of a pencil can be computed from its length and gravity, the fall of this wave should be computable from the wave itself. Linearizing the evolution at the balance point and taking the 20 eigenvalues, only two points lie outside the unit circle, both on the real axis, each doubly degenerate. The largest, μ₁ = 1.090086569, gives two predictions: a growth rate of 2 ln μ₁ = 0.172514 and an onset-time slope of 1/ln μ₁ = 11.593. The measurements: 0.172513 and 11.616. A value read straight from the time evolution, a value from varying the offset, a value computed at the balance point — three mutually independent measurements, explained by one and the same number. The onset of the expansion is the linear instability of a tilting pure wave. As a bonus, the falling directions are exactly two-dimensional; added to the original two, that makes four — the "rank 4" reported two articles ago was this sum.

After it stops, a different stage follows. The amplitudes of the ten waves become perfectly equal within a few hundred steps, and the phases take ten times longer to order themselves into four classes, 3+3+2+2. The settled shape comes with a theorem: the sum of squares vanishing at every vertex is equivalent to every vertex lying on the complex null cone, and together with equal amplitudes the settled state is a single geometric object, an equilateral complex simplex on the null cone. The 13 two-edge closures and 12 complete partitions that a numerical search had found for five bodies turn out to be necessities of this structure, 3×3 + 2×2 = 13 and 3!×2! = 12, and the 120 degrees for four bodies becomes a theorem too. Only the relative phase between the two distance families differs from run to run — a free direction (a modulus) that the dynamics leaves undetermined.

Finally, the most basic point. Auditing the program that builds the initial state back to its source, the closure condition is imposed nowhere. All it does is search for a self-consistent fixed point — find the eigenmode of a real antisymmetric matrix, feed its phases back, repeat. And the eigenmodes of a real antisymmetric matrix necessarily have zero sum of squares. Closure was not an axiom but a consequence. Self-consistency → complex rotating pair → zero sum of squares → phase running on a circle: this much is derived. But "returns after finitely many steps" requires a rational phase advance, and the mechanism selecting it has not been derived. The axioms went from two to one; the remaining one is the next problem.

The paper was written with generation and review assigned to different AIs, and the errors caught in review — an apparent contradiction in the regression statistics, a wrong explanation of the double eigenvalue, a deferred 6.8 % discrepancy that turned out to be settled by existing data — are corrected and recorded in the text. A correct answer is worth more than an expected one. I do not claim this is the inflation of the real universe; with the total conserved exactly, amplitude moving exponentially from one mode into others and a long ordering after saturation, the picture is closer to preheating than to inflation itself, as the discussion says.
