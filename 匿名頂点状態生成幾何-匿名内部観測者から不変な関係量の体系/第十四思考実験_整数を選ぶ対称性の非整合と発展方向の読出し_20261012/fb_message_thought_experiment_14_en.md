Why don't the three 137s agree exactly? Searching for pairs that are close but never lock

Details in the comments

Noether's theorem tells us that if there is a symmetry, there is a conserved quantity. But it does not tell us why that symmetry is there
Three of my papers produced values near α⁻¹, 137, 137.036010988… and 137.036042915…, from completely different lines of thought
All three differ from the observed 137.035999177 at the fifth or sixth decimal place, so I cannot call it agreement
If they agreed exactly, the two structures would lock completely and nothing would happen; if they were unrelated, they would not affect each other
When they are close but not identical, they may pull each other in without ever locking, producing metastability: long stays and sudden slips
The hypothesis: this looks to us like the ticks of time and space, discreteness and uncertainty, and the observed constants are that history read from one direction
In mathematics, integers are selected only when two conditions are imposed at once: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 for crystals, 1, 2, 4, 8 for number systems, 8 for E₈
The three 137s also share 8 and 128 with E₈, but the shapes inside differ; close again, but not identical
So what to search for are pairs that look theoretically unrelated, sit close to each other, and pull in without ever locking completely
If a reason for an exact match is found, this hypothesis loses
