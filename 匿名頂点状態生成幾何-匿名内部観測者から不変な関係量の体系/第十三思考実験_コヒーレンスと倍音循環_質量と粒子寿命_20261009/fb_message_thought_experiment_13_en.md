Why does a particle that has acquired mass remain instead of vanishing? The harmonics that seemed to flow out were coming back

Details in the comments

Coherence is not a property of the wave itself but a relation between the partner wave and the readout window
A localized wave made of N aligned harmonics has a peak width of 1/N; two sharp waves have only a narrow window to meet, in space and in time
With scattering, a particle's harmonics move even into a single-tone background; then why does a particle that has acquired mass not lose its harmonics and vanish?
I re-ran the July closed two-wave scattering experiment with the original program unchanged: there is no dissipation, and localization goes back and forth between the two waves
Each wave keeps strength 1 while its peak height changes by about 30 times, and the sum of the effective orders is always 33; equal division at 90°, swap at 180°, return at 360°
R = 0.70 was the value at which a coarse sweep showed the cleanest response; later fine sweeps found that the centre of the peak converges to the finite-order root R = cos²(23π/124)
With R set to this value, the waves swap completely at collision 62 and the state itself returns at collision 124; two runs matched bit for bit in every file
The period 124, however, is a consequence of the R that was put in; it does not show that the period arose from the interaction
It is not stable because it has a period; first there is an interaction, and a period appears in what remains stably. I think in this order
Next, I ask whether mass, clock slowing, length contraction, lifetimes and particle species can be read from a single interaction
