Where does 1/137 come from? I reread time, mass, charge and symmetry from the light-cone equation

Details in the comments

In the previous, eleventh thought experiment I put in the fine-structure constant α ≈ 1/137 as the measured value; this time I started from "what is α?"
In the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom α = v/c; 1/137 is, first of all, the ratio of the electron's speed to the speed of light
The Lorentz factor γ = 1/√(1−α²) is only the side ratio t/τ of r²+τ²+(it)²=0, the light-cone equation x²+y²+z²+(it)²=0 with one proper-time axis τ added
A particle slower than light, too, rides on the surface of the light cone once one axis is added
The same right triangle is the central projection ℓ²=|x|²+R_c², with r↔|x|, τ↔R_c (the curvature radius in the projection direction) and t↔ℓ
The equation in which squares add up to zero, which I have been calling the "zero closure", is not a special hypothesis but the light-cone equation itself
Extending to eight axes (x, y, z, t, R, Q₁, Q₂, Q₃) and taking off their names, directions that cannot be read are seen only as the length of a sum of squares
Symmetry is the transformations an observer cannot tell apart; an observer reading only t sees an O(7)-type symmetry
Splitting the five hidden directions into 3 and 2 gives SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) as a candidate (the same split as SU(5) grand unification); this is not a derivation
Which directions α is a ratio of remains unresolved; next I will check by computation whether changing only the directions that are read shows a different physics
