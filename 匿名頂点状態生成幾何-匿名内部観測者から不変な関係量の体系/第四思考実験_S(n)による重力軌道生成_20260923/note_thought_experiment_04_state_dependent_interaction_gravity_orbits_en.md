# Repeating the Same Action Was Not Enough - When I Let the Action Itself Change with the State, Nine Gravitational Orbits All Fitted, the Fourth Thought Experiment

## The twist left from the previous articles

In the second thought experiment, I applied one and the same action, again and again, to two values whose meaning had not yet been decided.

Out of that came orbits obeying an inverse-square force.

There is an internal state, there is an interaction, and an orbit comes out.

That was the direction.

In the third thought experiment the direction was reversed.

Orbits were computed in advance with the known equations of gravity, and I examined whether the mass ratio, the distance and the time could be read out from the observational data.

The readout worked.

But one dissatisfaction remained.

The orbit itself had not come out of an internal interaction.

The generation of the most complex motion was still outsourced to the known equations.

That is the question this time.

Can the gravitational orbits given from outside in the third experiment be brought back to an internal interaction?

---

## A fixed action was not enough

Orbits in a strong field have two features that a simple elliptical orbit does not have.

One is that the periastron turns a little at each revolution.

The other is that the orbit itself shrinks as gravitational waves are emitted.

If one tries to represent these while keeping the way of writing the state and the readout of the second thought experiment, and while repeating the same action, then these two changes have to be supplied by hand from outside the action, at every processing step.

That is the same as giving the orbit from outside, as in the third experiment.

The dissatisfaction would only come back in a different shape.

---

## I relaxed exactly one thing

At this point I did not go in the direction of adding states, or adding readouts.

I relaxed exactly one thing.

The assumption that the action is fixed.

The action itself was made a function of the present relational state.

At every processing step, the action changes.

But the rule for making the action does not change. From one and the same rule, the action at each step is determined.

One more thing was made clear.

The processing number is not a time.

It is only the index of which interaction step it is. The physical time is read out separately, from the area clock.

---

## The area stays preserved

The way the action is made is only a combination of a rotation and a stretch.

If one direction is stretched, the other is shrunk by the same factor.

In this way the area is preserved exactly at every processing step.

For the conservative part I used a known solution, the bound orbits of the Schwarzschild spacetime, and rewrote the progression of its angle as a recurrence that can be carried to any order.

For the dissipative part I used the same gravitational-wave reaction as in the third thought experiment, letting the two quantities that describe the shape of the orbit change a little at every step.

---

## Being able to make one orbit is not a result

This is the point I was most careful about this time.

Given one orbit, if you compute the action backwards step by step so as to match it, you can build a sequence that reproduces that orbit.

But that is only an encoding of the orbit. It is not obtaining a law of interaction.

So I put the criterion somewhere else.

With the same rule for making the action held fixed, can it be moved to a different orbit by changing only the initial conditions?

If it cannot be moved, then that rule was a contrivance for that one orbit.

---

## It fitted nine orbits as it was

First I tried the same strong-field condition as the third thought experiment.

Adding the gravitational-wave reaction brought the radial discrepancy against the reference orbit down from 2.51 per cent to 1.95 per cent.

Next, without changing the rule at all, I applied it to nine conditions: three sizes of orbit and three degrees of flattening.

The discrepancies over the nine were 0.508 per cent at the median, 0.832 per cent on average, and 2.864 per cent in the worst condition.

The weaker the field, the systematically smaller the discrepancy. In the weakest condition all three were below 0.21 per cent.

Then, holding the shape of the orbit fixed, I changed only the mass ratio of the two bodies: 1, 2, 4 and 10.

The discrepancy went from 0.508 per cent to 0.438 per cent. It did not increase.

What matters is not the accuracy itself but that the error did not run wild from case to case.

It varied smoothly with the strength of the field and the degree of flattening.

---

## Conserved inside, dissipative outside

One interesting thing is happening.

Inside, the area is preserved exactly at every processing step. Preserved to the limit of numerical precision.

Meanwhile the orbit that is read out is shrinking, emitting gravitational waves.

In other words, the internal state update preserving area and the observed orbit being non-dissipative are not the same thing.

Without building dissipation in directly, as a loss of internal area, motion that looks dissipative can be represented by the combination of a sequence of actions that change with the state and the readout.

Saying that this is the same as what the gravitational-wave reaction really is in nature, however, goes beyond the scope of this work.

---

## What I am not saying

Let me state clearly what these results do not say.

General relativity has not been derived from this action. The conservative part is built from a known solution, and the dissipative part uses a known expression.

Letting the action change with the state is itself an ordinary form in discrete dynamics. Nothing new was done as mathematics.

The way of making the action is not determined uniquely either. There is a freedom that the state and the next state alone do not fix, and here I used one choice among those, the one in which the area is preserved.

Nor have I proved that a fixed action can never work. Under this way of writing the state and this readout, removing the fixedness was the minimal change: that is the result of the search.

The remaining discrepancies are written down as they are.

The amount by which the orbit shrinks stays at about 92 per cent of the reference in every condition. About 8 per cent is missing. I take this to be because the simplest approximation is used for updating the shape of the orbit.

---

## What was found

Joined into one line, it comes out like this.

Repeating one and the same action was not enough to reach relativistic gravitational orbits.

When the action itself was allowed to change with the state, one and the same rule for making it fitted, as it was, a family of orbits differing in field strength, in flattening and in mass ratio.

This does not mean that this action is what gravity really is.

For the question this research set out with - can physical motion be generated from the sequential interaction of states and relations? - it means that the minimal change of giving up fixedness looks effective.

The next problem is whether the rule for making the action, assembled here from a known solution and a known expression, can be derived from a smaller number of relational quantities.
